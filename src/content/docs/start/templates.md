Professionals don't start from a blank page. They start from a *template they've refined a dozen times* and then tweak it for the client in front of them. That's not lazy — it's how you deliver a ₹15,000-looking proposal in twenty minutes instead of a nervous afternoon.

This page is the library. The table below indexes every template the course hands you and where it lives, so you can jump straight to it. Below the table, three of the most-used swipe files are printed in full, ready to copy, paste and adapt. Steal freely.

:::note[📋 Swipe this]
**How to use a template without sounding like a template:** change the specifics (business name, locality, real numbers), cut anything that doesn't apply, and read it aloud once. If it sounds like a human who's done this before, ship it. If it sounds like a form letter, you left the scaffolding in.
:::

## Full swipe file #1 — The one-page client proposal

Keep proposals to a single page. Owners don't read ten-page decks; they read the price and the scope. This closes gigs because it's clear, not clever.

:::note[📋 Swipe this]
```
PROPOSAL — Getting more customers from Google
Prepared for: [Business name], [locality]
Prepared by: [Your name] · [phone/email] · [date]

THE GOAL (in your words)
You told me you want more [appointment calls / walk-ins / enquiries]
from people nearby searching on Google. This plan does exactly that.

WHAT I FOUND (quick pre-audit)
- Google Business Profile: [unclaimed / incomplete / missing photos]
- Website: [slow on mobile / no analytics / thin content]
- Reviews: [only X reviews vs competitors' Y]
- Ads: [none running]
(Full audit included in month 1.)

WHAT I'LL DO
Month 1 — Foundations
  • Claim & fully optimise your Google Business Profile
  • Install analytics so we can SEE calls, forms and directions
  • Fix the highest-impact website issues
Month 2 — Visibility
  • Optimise your key pages for what customers actually search
  • Set up a simple system to collect more real reviews
  • [Optional] Launch a small, tracked Google Ads campaign
Ongoing — Grow & report
  • Improve what's working, cut what isn't
  • One clear monthly report: what happened and what's next

WHAT YOU GET EACH MONTH
- Visible progress on Google Search & Maps
- A plain-English report (no jargon)
- A direct line to me on WhatsApp

INVESTMENT
Setup (one-time): ₹[____]
Monthly retainer: ₹[____] /month
[If ads: your ad budget is separate and paid directly to Google.]

WHAT I NEED FROM YOU
- Access to your Google Business Profile & website
- Answers to a short list of questions
- Approval before anything goes live

Sound good? Reply "yes" and I'll send the access steps today.
```
:::

:::note[💰 Rupee check]
Leave the ₹ blanks and fill them per client. **Illustrative ranges for a local SMB:** one-time setup ₹5,000–₹20,000; monthly retainer ₹8,000–₹25,000; the client's *ad budget* (paid to Google, not you) commonly ₹10,000–₹30,000/month to start. These are examples, not fixed rates — they move with your experience, the city and the scope. Price the *value* (more calls), not the hours.
:::

## Full swipe file #2 — The review-request script (WhatsApp/SMS)

More real reviews is one of the fastest local-ranking and trust wins there is. The trick: ask a *genuinely happy* customer, right after a good experience, with a link that lands them straight on the review box.

:::note[📋 Swipe this]
**WhatsApp / SMS — send within a day of a good visit:**
```
Hi [Name], thanks for visiting [Business]! 🙏
If we did a good job today, a quick Google review would
mean a lot — it genuinely helps other people nearby find us.
Takes 30 seconds: [your GBP review short link]
Thank you so much!
— [Owner/Business name]
```

**Even shorter version:**
```
Hi [Name]! Glad we could help. 🙂 A quick Google review helps
us a lot — 30 seconds here: [review link]. Thank you!
```

**For the owner, to say in person at checkout:**
"If you were happy today, would you mind leaving us a quick Google
review? I'll send you the link on WhatsApp — it really helps us."
```
:::

:::caution[⚠️ Gotcha]
**Never** offer a discount, freebie or incentive in exchange for a review, and never write reviews yourself or in bulk. That violates Google's policies, and fake reviews get detected and removed — sometimes taking the listing's credibility with them. Ask real customers for honest reviews after real, good experiences. The only ethical (and durable) growth lever is *deserving* the review and *asking* for it. Get the client the "review short link" from inside their Business Profile so the customer lands directly on the star box.
:::

## Full swipe file #3 — The monthly report skeleton

The monthly report is what keeps you *retained*. It's not for you — it's for an owner who doesn't understand analytics and just wants to know: did this work, and what's next? Lead with outcomes, bury the jargon.

:::note[📋 Swipe this]
```
MONTHLY REPORT — [Business name]
Month: [Month, Year]   Prepared by: [Your name]

1. THE HEADLINE (one sentence)
   "This month you got [X] calls and [Y] enquiries from Google —
   [up/down] from [last month]."

2. THE NUMBERS THAT MATTER (a small table)
   Metric                     This month   Last month   Change
   Calls from Google              __           __        ↑/↓
   Website enquiries/forms        __           __        ↑/↓
   WhatsApp taps                  __           __        ↑/↓
   Directions requests            __           __        ↑/↓
   [If ads] Leads from ads        __           __        ↑/↓
   [If ads] Cost per lead      ₹__          ₹__          ↑/↓

3. WHAT I DID THIS MONTH (3–5 plain bullets)
   • [e.g. Added 12 photos and posted 4 updates to your profile]
   • [e.g. Rewrote your "root canal" page — now ranking on page 1]
   • [e.g. Collected 6 new 5-star reviews]

4. WHAT'S WORKING / WHAT ISN'T (honest, short)
   Working: [____]
   Watching: [____]  (and what I'll do about it)

5. NEXT MONTH'S PLAN (3 bullets)
   • [____]
   • [____]
   • [____]

6. ANYTHING I NEED FROM YOU
   • [e.g. Approve the new offer post / send 3 clinic photos]

Questions? WhatsApp me anytime.
```
:::

:::tip[⚔️ War story]
A freelancer sent a beautiful 14-tab analytics dashboard every month and still lost the client. Why? The owner never understood a single tab and quietly assumed nothing was happening. The freelancer who replaced him sent *this* one-page skeleton — headline first, five plain bullets — and kept the retainer for two years. Report *outcomes in the owner's language*, not metrics in yours. The dashboard is your workbench; the one-pager is what you hand across the table. *(Composite, but you'll see this exact mistake in the wild.)*
:::

<details>
<summary>Q: Why keep client proposals to a single page?</summary>

Because SMB owners don't read ten-page decks — they read the scope and the price. A clear one-pager that names their goal, what you'll do, and what it costs closes more gigs than an impressive-looking deck they'll never finish. Clarity converts; length intimidates.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q: A client asks you to offer customers ₹100 off for leaving a 5-star review. What do you say?</summary>

No — that violates Google's review policies, and incentivised or fake reviews get removed and can damage the listing's credibility. Instead, ask genuinely happy customers for honest reviews right after a good experience, with a direct review link. Deserve it, then ask for it.
</details>
