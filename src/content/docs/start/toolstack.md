Here's a secret the agencies won't put on their pitch decks: the entire craft of local SEO, analytics and Google Ads can be run on tools that cost **₹0**. Not a trial. Not a crippled freemium teaser. Genuinely free, made by Google, used by professionals every day.

That's the whole thesis of this page. You do not need a ₹8,000/month tool subscription to land your first client. You need a Google account, a browser, and the discipline to actually open these tools. The paid stuff comes *later* — when a client's income is paying for it, not yours.

Bookmark this page. It's the reference you'll come back to every module.

:::note[💰 Rupee check]
**The core stack in this course costs ₹0.** Google Analytics 4, Search Console, Tag Manager, Looker Studio, Business Profile, Keyword Planner, Trends, PageSpeed Insights and the Rich Results Test are all free, forever, for the work you'll do. The only "cost" is that some (like Keyword Planner) want you to have a Google Ads account — which is free to *create*; you're only charged if you actually run ads. Paid research tools (Ahrefs/Semrush/Moz) are a *later* upgrade, illustrative ₹8,000–₹18,000/month, and you buy them when a client's retainer covers them — never before.
:::

## Measurement — how you prove you did something

This is the group that separates a professional from someone who "did some SEO." If you can't measure it, you can't invoice for it.

| Tool | What it does | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Google Analytics 4 (GA4)** | Tracks what people do *on the website* — pages viewed, key events (form fills, calls, WhatsApp taps), where they came from. The modern Google analytics; replaced the retired Universal Analytics. | Free |
| **Google Search Console (GSC)** | Shows how the site performs *in Google Search* — which queries brought impressions and clicks, average position, indexing problems. Your SEO scoreboard. | Free |
| **Google Tag Manager (GTM)** | A container that fires your tracking tags (GA4, conversion tags) without editing site code every time. Learn it once, stop bothering the developer. | Free |
| **Looker Studio** | Turns GA4 + GSC + Ads data into a clean, shareable dashboard the client can actually understand. This is what makes your monthly report look ₹15,000-worthy. | Free |

:::note[🎓 Foundations]
**GA4 ≠ the website.** GA4 *watches* the website. Search Console *watches Google's results.* They answer different questions — "what happened on the site" vs "what happened in search" — and you'll use both. Beginners constantly conflate them; don't.
:::

## Local — the map pack money-maker

For a dentist, gym or boutique, this is where the phone actually rings. Local search is the highest-ROI work you'll do for an Indian SMB.

| Tool | What it does | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Google Business Profile (GBP)** | The free listing that puts a business on Google Maps and in the local "map pack." Claiming and optimising this is often the single biggest win. (This is the current brand — the old "Google My Business" app was retired; you manage it from Search and Maps now.) | Free |
| **Google Maps** | Where the GBP shows up, gets reviews, gets directions taps. You'll live here for local work. | Free |
| **JustDial / Sulekha / IndiaMART** | India's big local directories and citation sources. Consistent listings here reinforce the business's Name-Address-Phone (NAP) and drive their own leads. | Free listings (they upsell paid ads) |
| **Bing Places** | Bing's version of GBP. Small traffic, but free, quick, and a legitimate citation. Ten-minute job. | Free |

:::caution[⚠️ Gotcha]
The directories will *phone your client relentlessly* trying to sell premium placements once a listing goes up. That's their business model, not a requirement. A free, accurate, consistent listing does the citation job. Tell your client to expect the calls and to say no.
:::

## Keywords & research — reading demand

You can't rank for words nobody searches, and you can't pitch a plan without knowing what people actually type.

| Tool | What it does | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Google Keyword Planner** | Google's own keyword volume and idea tool. Lives inside Google Ads. Great for "how many people search 'root canal cost Jayanagar'." | Free (needs a Google Ads account) |
| **Google Trends** | Shows relative interest over time and by region — perfect for spotting seasonal and city-level demand ("AC repair" spikes pre-summer). | Free |
| **Autocomplete & People-Also-Ask** | Google's own suggestions as you type, and the "related questions" box on results pages. A free, live map of real phrasing and questions. | Free |
| **Answer/question research** | Tools that cluster the real questions people ask around a topic, so your content answers them. Use free tiers. | Free tier |

:::caution[⚠️ Gotcha]
Without an active ad spend, Keyword Planner often shows **volume as a range** (e.g. "100–1K") instead of an exact number. That's normal and fine for direction. Don't pay for anything just to see a tighter number — the range tells you enough to prioritise.
:::

## Ads (SEM) — buying attention while you earn it

| Tool | What it does | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Google Ads** | Run search/display/Performance Max campaigns. Free to open an account; you pay only for the clicks/spend you choose. | Account free; you set the budget |
| **Ads conversion tracking** | Tells Ads which clicks turned into leads (calls, forms), so you optimise for *outcomes*, not traffic. Non-negotiable before scaling spend. | Free |
| **Ads Transparency Center** | Google's public library of ads currently running. Spy on what competitors are advertising — legally and for free. | Free |

## Technical — is the site even in the race?

| Tool | What it does | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **PageSpeed Insights** | Grades a page's speed and Core Web Vitals on mobile and desktop, with fixes. Google's own tool. | Free |
| **Rich Results Test / schema validators** | Checks whether your structured data (schema) is valid and eligible for rich results. | Free |
| **Mobile-friendly checks** | Confirms a page renders and works on mobile — where nearly all local search happens in India. | Free |
| **Screaming Frog SEO Spider** | A desktop crawler that audits titles, meta, broken links, redirects across a whole site. The free tier crawls up to **500 URLs**, which covers almost every SMB site you'll meet. | Free tier (500-URL limit) |

## Content & AI — your fast, fallible intern

| Tool | What it does | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **An AI writing assistant** | Drafts outlines, first passes, meta descriptions, FAQ answers — *fast*. It is a **drafting intern, not an autopilot.** You verify every fact and rewrite in a human voice. | Free tier available |
| **A plagiarism / fact sanity check** | A quick pass to catch duplicated text and, more importantly, *hallucinated facts* the AI invented. | Free tier available |

:::tip[⚔️ War story]
A freelancer let an AI write a dentist's service page unedited. It confidently listed a treatment the clinic **doesn't offer** and quoted a price the dentist never set. The owner spotted it, trust cracked, and the gig ended in Module 2. AI drafts; *you* are responsible for every word that ships. Treat it like a brilliant intern who occasionally lies with total confidence.
:::

## Optional paid tools — the "when the income justifies it" tier

You do not need these to start. You'll *want* some once you're managing multiple clients and your time is worth more than the subscription.

| Tool | What it adds | Illustrative cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Ahrefs / Semrush / Moz** | Deep backlink data, competitor research, rank tracking, keyword databases — faster than stitching free tools together. | ~₹8,000–₹18,000/month each |
| **Ahrefs Webmaster Tools** | A *free* slice of Ahrefs for sites you verify — backlink and site-audit basics. | Free tier |
| **Bing Webmaster Tools** | Bing's free Search-Console equivalent, with its own keyword and crawl data. | Free |

:::tip[🚀 Level up]
The pro move isn't owning every tool — it's knowing which *one* paid tool removes your biggest bottleneck. Doing lots of backlink and competitor analysis? One Ahrefs/Semrush seat pays for itself. Mostly local GBP work? You may never need it. Buy the tool that buys back your *time*, and bill it to the client work that requires it.
:::

## Accounts to create first (do this before Module 1)

A tidy setup now saves hours later. Ideally, create these under a **dedicated Google account for your freelance work** — not your personal Gmail — so client access stays clean and separable.

- [ ] A dedicated Google account for your SEO business.
- [ ] **Google Analytics 4** — you'll create properties per client inside it.
- [ ] **Google Search Console** — verify each client site here.
- [ ] **Google Tag Manager** — one container per client site.
- [ ] **Google Ads** — free to open; unlocks Keyword Planner even before you spend.
- [ ] **Looker Studio** — nothing to set up beyond signing in; you'll build reports here.
- [ ] **Bing Webmaster Tools + Bing Places** — quick free wins and citations.
- [ ] A note/password manager entry to keep every client's access organised.

:::note[📋 Swipe this]
**One-line rule for tool access:** *"You (the client) own every account; I get manager/admin access."* Create GA4, Search Console and Ads under the **client's** ownership where possible, and add yourself as a user. When the gig ends, they keep their data and you keep your reputation. Never lock a client out of their own analytics — it's the fastest way to earn a bad review.
:::

<details>
<summary>Q: Your client asks, "which of these do I have to pay for to get started?"</summary>

None of the core stack. GA4, Search Console, Tag Manager, Looker Studio, Business Profile, Keyword Planner, Trends and PageSpeed Insights are all free. A Google Ads account is free to *open* (needed for Keyword Planner); you only pay when you choose to run ads. Paid research suites like Ahrefs/Semrush are optional upgrades for later.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q: What's the difference between Google Analytics 4 and Google Search Console?</summary>

GA4 measures behaviour *on the website* (pages, events, conversions, traffic sources). Search Console measures performance *in Google Search* (queries, impressions, clicks, position, indexing). Different tools for different questions — you use both, and confusing them marks you as a beginner.
</details>
