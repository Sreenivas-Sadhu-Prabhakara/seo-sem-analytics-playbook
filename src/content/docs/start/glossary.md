Every trade hides behind vocabulary, and search marketing hides behind more than most — a wall of acronyms designed to make simple ideas sound expensive. This page tears that wall down. Whenever a term ambushes you in a lesson, come back here.

Definitions are grouped by theme so related ideas sit together. Plain English, one or two lines each, occasionally a joke — but always accurate. Skim the group you need; nobody reads a glossary front to back (and if you do, we salute you).

## The big picture

**SEO (Search Engine Optimisation)** — Earning *unpaid* visibility in search results by making a site genuinely more relevant, trustworthy and usable. The slow-cooked, compounding half of the game.

**SEM (Search Engine Marketing)** — The umbrella for getting visibility from search, most often used to mean the *paid* side (Google Ads). If SEO is farming, SEM is buying vegetables at the market — faster, but you pay each time.

**PPC (Pay-Per-Click)** — The pricing model behind most search ads: you pay only when someone actually clicks. No click, no charge.

**Organic vs paid** — *Organic* results are the unpaid listings you earn through SEO. *Paid* results are ads you buy. On a results page, ads sit up top (labelled "Sponsored"); organic sits below.

**SERP (Search Engine Results Page)** — The page you get after searching. A modern SERP is a crowded bazaar: ads, the map pack, "People also ask," images, and finally the classic blue links.

## Keywords & intent

**Keyword** — A word or phrase people type into search. Your job is to know which ones your client's customers actually use ("dentist near me"), not which ones sound impressive.

**Search intent** — *Why* someone searched. Are they researching ("root canal meaning"), comparing, or ready to act ("root canal cost Jayanagar")? Match the page to the intent or you'll rank for the wrong crowd.

**Long-tail** — Longer, more specific, lower-volume searches ("emergency kids dentist Indiranagar Sunday"). Fewer people, but far more ready to buy — and much easier to rank for.

## Search results & local

**Map pack / local pack** — The box of three local business listings with a map that appears for local searches. For an SMB, landing here is often the single biggest win — it's prime real estate above most organic links.

**GBP (Google Business Profile)** — The free business listing that powers your presence on Google Maps and in the map pack. (The current name; the old "Google My Business" app was retired — you manage it from Search and Maps now.)

**NAP (Name, Address, Phone)** — A business's core contact details. The rule: keep them *identical* everywhere online. Inconsistent NAP confuses Google and erodes local ranking.

**Citation** — A mention of a business's NAP on another site (JustDial, Sulekha, IndiaMART, Bing Places). Consistent citations reinforce that the business is real and where it says it is.

## Links & authority

**Backlink** — A link from another website to yours. Think of it as a vote of confidence; a few links from relevant, trusted sites beat a thousand spammy ones.

**Anchor text** — The clickable words a link is wrapped around. "Best dentist in Indiranagar" tells Google more about the destination than "click here" ever will.

**Nofollow** — A tag on a link telling search engines "don't pass ranking credit through this." Used for ads, paid and untrusted links.

**DA / authority** — "Domain Authority" and similar scores are *third-party estimates* (by tools like Moz/Ahrefs) of how strong a site is, not an official Google metric. Useful for comparison, dangerous if you treat it as gospel.

## On-page & content

**On-page** — Everything you optimise *on the page itself*: titles, headings, content, internal links, images. The stuff fully within your control.

**Off-page** — Signals from *outside* the site: backlinks, citations, reviews, mentions. Harder to control, which is exactly why they carry weight.

**Title tag** — The clickable headline of a search result and the browser-tab text. A page with no title tag is a shop with no name board. High-leverage, often neglected.

**Meta description** — The short summary under a search result. Google may rewrite it, but a good one is a free ad that lifts your click-through rate.

**H1** — The main on-page heading (there should be one clear one per page). It tells readers and search engines what the page is about.

**Schema / structured data** — Extra code that labels page content for search engines ("this is a review / a price / an FAQ"). It doesn't change what users see directly but can unlock rich results.

**Rich result** — An enhanced search listing — star ratings, FAQs, images — made possible by valid schema. More eye-catching, so more clicks.

**Service-page** — A page dedicated to one service the business offers ("Root Canal Treatment"). Focused pages targeting real search intent convert far better than one page that lists everything.

## Technical SEO

**Technical SEO** — Making sure search engines can *find, read and trust* a site: speed, mobile-friendliness, crawlability, structured data. The plumbing; nobody notices it until it leaks.

**Crawling** — When a search engine's bot follows links and fetches pages to see what's out there. If it can't crawl a page, that page effectively doesn't exist.

**Indexing** — When a crawled page is stored in Google's index and becomes *eligible* to show in results. Crawled ≠ indexed; both must happen.

**robots.txt** — A file telling crawlers which parts of a site they may or may not access. Powerful and dangerous — one wrong line can hide a whole site.

**Sitemap** — An XML file listing a site's important URLs to help search engines discover them. A helpful map, not a magic ranking button.

**Canonical** — A tag that names the "official" version of a page when duplicates or near-duplicates exist, so Google knows which one to rank.

**noindex** — A tag telling search engines *not* to include a page in results. Handy for thank-you pages; catastrophic if left on a page you want to rank.

**Core Web Vitals** — Google's page-experience metrics for real-world speed and stability:
- **LCP (Largest Contentful Paint)** — how fast the main content loads. Slow LCP = users bounce before your page even shows up.
- **INP (Interaction to Next Paint)** — how quickly the page responds when tapped. (Replaced the older FID metric.)
- **CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift)** — how much the layout jumps around while loading. High CLS is why you tap "buy" and hit an ad instead.

## Analytics & measurement

**GA4 (Google Analytics 4)** — Google's current analytics platform, built around *events*. It measures what people do on the website. (It replaced the retired Universal Analytics — if a guide mentions "sessions and bounce rate the old way," it's out of date.)

**Key event / conversion** — An action you've flagged as valuable — a call, a form submit, a WhatsApp tap. In GA4 these are marked as "key events." Everything you do is ultimately about producing more of them.

**GTM (Google Tag Manager)** — A container that lets you add and fire tracking tags (GA4, ad conversions) without editing site code each time. Learn it once, stop pestering the developer.

**Search Console (GSC)** — Google's free tool showing how a site performs *in Search*: queries, impressions, clicks, position and indexing issues. Your SEO scoreboard.

**Impressions** — How many times a listing was *shown* in search results. Being seen, not necessarily clicked.

**CTR (Click-Through Rate)** — Clicks ÷ impressions, as a percentage. A low CTR on a high position usually means a weak title/description.

**Position** — Your average ranking spot for a query in Search Console. Position 1 is the top organic result; the map pack and ads sit separately.

**Bounce / engagement rate** — In GA4 the headline metric flipped to *engagement rate* — the share of sessions that were "engaged" (long enough, multi-page, or converting). Bounce rate is now roughly its inverse. A "bounce" isn't always bad — someone who got your phone number and left got what they came for.

**Attribution** — The logic for deciding which touchpoints get *credit* for a conversion. It's how you answer "did the ad or the SEO cause this call?" — and it's genuinely hard.

**Last-click** — The simplest attribution model: 100% of the credit goes to the final click before conversion. Easy to read, but it ignores everything that warmed the customer up earlier.

**UTM** — Little tags you add to a URL (`?utm_source=...`) so analytics knows exactly which campaign, source or post a visitor came from. Label your links or you'll be guessing.

**Looker Studio** — Google's free dashboard tool that turns GA4, Search Console and Ads data into a clean, shareable report. What makes your monthly deliverable look professional.

## Google Ads (SEM)

**Quality Score** — Google's 1–10 estimate of how relevant and useful your ad, keyword and landing page are. Higher score = lower cost and better positions. Google rewarding you for not wasting users' time.

**Ad Rank** — What actually decides ad position: your bid × quality and expected impact. You can outrank a higher bidder by being more relevant — money isn't everything.

**Match type** — How loosely a keyword can trigger your ad: *broad*, *phrase*, or *exact*. Broad reaches more but wastes budget on junk; exact is tight but narrow.

**Negative keyword** — A term you *block* so your ad never shows for it (e.g. "free," "jobs," "salary"). Your first line of defence against burning budget on the wrong searches.

**CPC (Cost Per Click)** — What you pay for one click. Varies wildly by industry and competition.

**CPA (Cost Per Acquisition)** — What you pay, on average, for one conversion (a lead or sale). More meaningful than CPC — clicks don't pay bills, customers do.

**CPL (Cost Per Lead)** — CPA's local-service flavour: the cost of one enquiry (a call or form). The number an SMB owner actually feels.

**ROAS (Return On Ad Spend)** — Revenue earned ÷ ad money spent. A ROAS of 4 means ₹4 back for every ₹1 spent. The verdict on whether ads are worth it.

**Conversion rate** — The share of visitors (or clicks) that take the action you wanted. Doubling conversion rate is often cheaper than doubling traffic.

**Responsive Search Ad (RSA)** — The standard Google search ad format: you supply multiple headlines and descriptions, and Google mixes the best-performing combinations automatically.

**Performance Max (PMax)** — A Google Ads campaign type that spends across all of Google's channels (Search, Maps, YouTube, Display, Gmail) using automation. Powerful, but a black box — watch it closely.

## Trust, quality & business

**E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness)** — The qualities Google's guidelines say make content trustworthy. Not a direct score, but a north star — especially for health, legal and money topics.

**YMYL (Your Money or Your Life)** — Topics that can affect someone's health, safety or finances (a dental clinic qualifies). Google holds these to a higher trust bar, so credibility signals matter more.

**Retainer** — A recurring monthly fee for ongoing work — the freelancer's holy grail, because it turns one sale into predictable income. The whole point of doing the capstone well is earning these.

<details>
<summary>Q: A page was crawled but isn't showing up in Google. Which term explains the gap?</summary>

Indexing. Crawling means Google *fetched* the page; indexing means it was *stored and made eligible* to appear in results. A page can be crawled and still not indexed — check Search Console for the reason (a stray noindex tag is a classic culprit).
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q: What's the practical difference between CPC, CPA and CPL?</summary>

CPC is the cost of a single *click*; CPA is the cost of a *conversion* (any valued action); CPL is the cost of a *lead* (an enquiry — a call or form). They get more meaningful as you go down the list: clicks don't pay bills, leads and customers do, so an SMB owner cares about CPL far more than CPC.
</details>
