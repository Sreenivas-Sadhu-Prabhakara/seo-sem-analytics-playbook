There are two ways to finish this course. The first: you read twelve modules, nod along, close the tab, and three weeks later you couldn't set up a GA4 conversion if your rent depended on it. The second: you attach every single lesson to one real business, and by Module 12 you're holding a live, optimised, *measurable* result you can show a paying client.

The difference between those two outcomes is one decision you make right now. It's called the **capstone client.**

## The core idea: one business, every module

In [Module 1](/seo-sem-analytics-playbook/modules/01-land-the-client/) you recruit **one real local business** to be your practice client. From Module 2 onward, every assignment is done *for them*. Not a hypothetical. Not a sandbox. Their actual Google Business Profile, their actual website, their actual phone that you're trying to make ring.

You do the audit for them. You set up analytics for them. You claim and optimise their GBP, write their service pages, run their first ad campaign, and build their monthly dashboard. Twelve modules later, you don't have notes — you have a **client and a case study.**

:::tip[⚔️ War story]
Two freelancers took the same course. Priya recruited her uncle's dental clinic in week one and did every assignment for it, warts and all — a real GBP she had to argue about with a real dentist. Rahul "didn't want to bother anyone yet" and did everything on a made-up business. Three months later Priya walked into her first paid pitch with a live listing and a real before/after chart. Rahul walked in with... a very nice PDF about a company that doesn't exist. Guess who got the ₹15,000/month retainer. *(Composite — but this pattern is depressingly consistent.)*
:::

## Why a real client beats theory, every time

- **Real constraints teach you.** A real dentist says "I don't want to discount," a real boutique owner has no logo file, a real site is on a platform you didn't expect. You learn to handle *reality*, which is the actual job.
- **Real data is the whole point.** Analytics on a fake site shows nothing. Analytics on a real business shows calls, forms, WhatsApp taps — the numbers that become your proof.
- **Real stakes build real skill.** When someone's actual business is on the line (even unpaid), you double-check your work. That pressure is the teacher.
- **It ends in a portfolio, not a certificate.** "I completed a course" impresses nobody. "I took this clinic from an unclaimed listing to 40 monthly calls" wins the next gig.

## Who makes a good capstone client

You are looking for a friend's or relative's small business with an obvious online gap and a willing owner. That's it. Perfect is the enemy of started.

:::note[📋 Swipe this]
**Capstone client shortlist — score a candidate on these:**

- [ ] **Real local business** with a real service people actually *search* for (dentist, gym, salon, coaching centre, boutique, restaurant, repair service, clinic).
- [ ] **Weak online presence** — unclaimed or thin GBP, a slow/old site or none, no ads, few reviews. (Good news: more room to show improvement.)
- [ ] **A willing, reachable owner** — someone who'll answer a WhatsApp, give you access, and let you experiment. A relative or friend is ideal.
- [ ] **Serves a specific area** — local intent ("near me", a locality name) is where you'll win fastest.
- [ ] **Okay with you learning on them** — set expectations that this is your practice project; you'll deliver real value but you're learning too.
- [ ] **Bonus:** they'd give you a testimonial or referral if you do well.

*Pick the candidate with the highest total. Don't overthink it — the willing uncle beats the perfect stranger.*
:::

:::caution[⚠️ Gotcha]
The number-one reason people fail this course: **waiting for the "perfect" client.** They spend three weeks hunting for an ideal business instead of texting the cousin with the boutique *today*. There is no perfect client. A willing, imperfect, reachable local business you can start on this week beats a dream client you'll never actually secure. Start rough. You can always apply the skills to a better client later — the skills are the asset.
:::

## No real client? The Simulated Client fallback

If you genuinely, truly cannot recruit one — you're between cities, you have no network yet, whatever — every assignment includes a **Simulated Client** fallback. It's Plan B, and you should use the *same* simulated business across all modules so your work compounds into one coherent case study.

Here's a fully-specified one you can adopt and reuse:

:::note[📋 Swipe this]
**Simulated Capstone Client — "GreenLeaf Dental", Indiranagar, Bengaluru** *(fictional composite — clearly hypothetical, safe to reuse across every module):*

- **Business:** A 2-chair dental clinic, run by Dr. Anjali Rao, open ~6 years. General dentistry, root canals, cleaning, braces, kids' dentistry.
- **Online presence:** Google Business Profile exists but is **unclaimed and incomplete** — wrong hours, no photos, three old reviews. Website is a **basic, slow, single-page site** from 2019, not mobile-optimised, no analytics installed. **No Google Ads, ever.**
- **The market:** People in Indiranagar search "dentist near me", "root canal cost Indiranagar", "kids dentist Bengaluru". Several competitors have polished listings and 100+ reviews.
- **The owner's goal, in her words:** *"I just want more appointment calls. I don't understand any of this online stuff."*
- **Budget:** Willing to spend a small ad budget (illustrative ₹10,000–₹15,000/month) *if* it clearly brings calls. Skeptical, practical, no patience for jargon.

Use these exact facts every time an assignment says "for your capstone client." Because the details are fixed, your Module 2 audit, Module 5 GBP work and Module 11 report will all line up into one story.
:::

Every module produces a real deliverable *for this client*. By the end they stack into a complete engagement. Here's the map (module themes are indicative — follow your sidebar for exact titles):

| **2 — Audit** | A 15-minute pre-audit and a full audit report of their current online presence. |

Treat the free gig as your paid gig's marketing budget. (Ranges are illustrative and move with the market — verify current local rates.)

## Module 12: it all becomes one case study

The finale isn't a quiz. In Module 12 you assemble everything — the before/after of their listing, the traffic and calls in GA4, the ad results, the review growth — into a single **portfolio case study.** That artefact is the most valuable thing you'll leave this course with. It's the difference between *saying* you can do SEO and *showing* it.

## Consent and privacy — don't skip this

You're touching a real person's business and, later, publishing results. Be a professional about it.

- **Get explicit consent** to use the client's name, logo and results in your public portfolio and case study. A one-line WhatsApp confirmation is enough — but get it.
- **Offer to anonymise.** If they'd rather not be named, present the case study as "a dental clinic in Bengaluru." The *numbers and process* are what impress; the name is optional.
- **Never fabricate results.** If calls went up 30%, say 30%. If a metric was flat, say so and explain what you learned. A single invented number, discovered later, torches your entire credibility. The whole value of your portfolio is that it's *true*.
- **Handle their data respectfully** — access accounts as their manager, don't share their private numbers publicly without permission, and hand everything back cleanly if you part ways.

Don't pitch a big engagement; just ask if you can practise on their online presence for free and see if you can get them more customers. That single message is the real start of this course.
