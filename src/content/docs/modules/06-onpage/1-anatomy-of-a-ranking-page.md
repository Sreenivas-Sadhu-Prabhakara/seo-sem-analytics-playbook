You open Revive Physiotherapy's "Services" page in a browser tab, then peek at the source. The title tag — the single most important line of on-page real estate on the entire site — reads: `Home | Welcome`.

Two words that tell Google nothing, tell a searcher nothing, and waste the biggest lever you'll touch all week. A physio clinic in Indiranagar with fifteen years of experience, and its title tag is a shop with no name board. This lesson is the anatomy of a page Google can actually read — element by element — so you never leave a lever like that on the floor again.

## Why this pays

On-page fundamentals are the highest-leverage, lowest-effort work in SEO. You're not building links or waiting six months for a domain to age — you're editing text on pages Google already crawls. Fix the title tags on a client's five money pages in an afternoon and you've often moved rankings before your invoice clears. Clients feel this fast, which makes it the easiest work to *sell*.

Think of a web page as a skeleton. Here are the bones, most to least load-bearing.

## The title tag — your single biggest lever

The `<title>` is the clickable blue headline in Google's results and the text in the browser tab. It's the strongest on-page signal of what a page is about, and it drives whether anyone clicks. Get this one thing right and you've done more than most "SEO experts" who charge triple your rate.

**The formula:** `Primary Keyword + Locality + Brand`

- ❌ `Home | Welcome`
- ✅ `Sports Injury Physiotherapy in Indiranagar | Revive Physio`
- ✅ `Back Pain Treatment in HSR Layout, Bengaluru | Revive`

Keep it roughly **50–60 characters** so Google doesn't truncate it with an `…`. Lead with the keyword a real person types. Put the locality in — "near me" searches resolve to place, and a title that names Indiranagar wins the Indiranagar searcher. Brand goes last; it earns trust but it isn't what they searched for.

:::note[📋 Swipe this]
**On-page checklist — run this on every page:**

1. **Title tag:** `Keyword + Locality | Brand`, 50–60 chars, unique to this page.
2. **Meta description:** 140–160 chars, benefit + locality + a call to action. Not a ranking factor — a *click* factor.
3. **One H1** that matches the page's promise.
4. **H2/H3s** in a logical outline (they're a table of contents, not decoration).
5. **URL slug:** short, lowercase, hyphenated, keyword-bearing.
6. **Keyword** in the first 100 words, naturally.
7. **Internal links** out to relevant service pages with descriptive anchor text.
8. **Images:** real filenames + alt text describing the image.
9. **Scannable:** short paragraphs, bullets, sub-heads. Passes the squint test.
:::

## The meta description — for clicks, not rankings

Here's the nuance that separates you from the amateurs: **the meta description is not a ranking factor** — and Google often rewrites it anyway. So why bother? Because when Google *does* show yours, it's ad copy under your headline; it decides click-through rate. Write it like a one-line pitch: benefit, locality, a nudge to act.

> `Sports injury? Our Indiranagar physios get athletes back to training with hands-on rehab. Book a same-week assessment — call or WhatsApp today.`

Around **140–160 characters**. Every service page gets its own — never one description copy-pasted sitewide.

## One H1, and a logical heading outline

The **H1** is the page's on-screen headline. **One per page.** It should echo the title tag's promise in human language: `Sports Injury Physiotherapy in Indiranagar`. Then **H2s and H3s** structure the body into a scannable outline — think of them as a table of contents both Google and a skimming reader use to navigate. On a service page that's naturally: the problem, your approach, what's included, proof, FAQs, book now.

:::caution[⚠️ Gotcha]
**Duplicate titles are a silent killer.** The laziest "SEO" move is stamping the same title tag — or the same H1 — across every page: "Revive Physiotherapy — Best Physio in Bengaluru" on all twelve pages. Now Google can't tell your back-pain page from your sports-injury page, and neither ranks well for either. **Every page needs a unique title, H1 and meta description.** Crawl the site (a free crawler like Screaming Frog on its free tier does this) and sort by title to catch duplicates fast.
:::

## URL slugs — short and readable

A URL is a tiny billboard. `revivephysio.in/sports-injury-physiotherapy-indiranagar` beats `revivephysio.in/page?id=42&cat=7` every time — it's readable, it carries the keyword, and people trust links they can read. Keep slugs **short, lowercase, hyphen-separated**, no stop-words salad. And **don't change live URLs casually** — a changed URL is a dead link unless you redirect it (that's a Module 7 conversation).

## Keyword placement without stuffing

Use your target keyword where it matters — title, H1, URL, first 100 words, one or two sub-heads, naturally through the copy — then **write for the human and stop counting.** Google's language understanding is good; it reads synonyms and context ("physio," "physiotherapist," "rehab," "back pain treatment") as one topic.

:::caution[⚠️ Gotcha]
**Keyword stuffing is a 2009 tactic that now actively hurts you.** "Physiotherapy Indiranagar — our physiotherapy Indiranagar clinic offers physiotherapy Indiranagar services by physiotherapy Indiranagar experts" reads like a ransom note, converts nobody, and trips spam signals. If you'd be embarrassed to read it aloud to the clinic owner, delete it.
:::

## Internal links — the free lever everyone forgets

When your blog post on "5 stretches for lower back pain" mentions treatment, **link the phrase "back pain physiotherapy" to the back-pain service page.** Internal links pass relevance and authority between pages and hand Google a map of your site. The **anchor text** (the clickable words) should describe the destination — never "click here." A well-linked ten-page site routinely out-ranks a stranded twenty-page one.

## Images — filenames and alt text

`IMG_20231104.jpg` tells Google nothing. Rename it `sports-injury-rehab-indiranagar.jpg` before upload, and write **alt text** that describes the image: `Physiotherapist treating a runner's knee at Revive Physio, Indiranagar`. Alt text is accessibility-first (screen readers read it aloud), a mild relevance signal, and how you show up in Google Images. Describe the picture honestly; don't cram keywords in.

## Readability & scannability

Nobody *reads* a service page — they scan it. Short paragraphs (2–3 lines), bullets, bold on the phrases that matter, sub-heads every screenful. On mobile, a wall of text is a bounce. If a stressed person with a sore back can't get the gist while standing in a lift, the page fails — no matter how "optimised" the title is.

## Common mistakes

- **Homepage doing every job.** One page trying to rank for physio, sports injury, back pain *and* geriatric care ranks for none. (Fix: dedicated pages — next lesson.)
- **Title tag left as the theme default** (`Home`, `Untitled`, the template name).
- **Meta descriptions blank or duplicated** sitewide.
- **Two or three H1s** because the theme styles big text as H1.

## Do this now

Open your capstone client's homepage and top service page. Note the current **title tag** and **H1** for each. Rewrite both titles using `Keyword + Locality | Brand` in 50–60 characters, and draft one fresh meta description per page. That's four lines of text — and often your fastest ranking win of the whole engagement.

<details>
<summary>Q: The meta description isn't a ranking factor — so why write a good one?</summary>

Because it's the ad copy under your headline in the results. Google may or may not use it, but when it does, it decides whether someone clicks your result or the competitor's. It drives click-through rate and conversions, not rank — and clicks are the whole point.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q: Your client's site uses the same title tag on all eight pages. What breaks?</summary>

Google can't distinguish the pages' topics, so each competes with itself and none ranks cleanly for its specific keyword. Give every page a unique title, H1 and meta description matched to that page's one job.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q: How many keywords should you cram into the copy for best results?</summary>

None extra — write naturally for the reader. Place the target term in the title, H1, URL and first 100 words, then stop counting. Modern Google understands synonyms and context; stuffing reads badly and can trigger spam signals.
</details>
