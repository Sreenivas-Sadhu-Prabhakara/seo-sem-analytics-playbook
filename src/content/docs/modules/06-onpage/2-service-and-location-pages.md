Revive Physiotherapy has one page called "Services." On it: a paragraph that says *"We offer a wide range of physiotherapy services for all your needs"* followed by a bulleted list of nine treatments, each two words long. That single page is trying to rank for "sports injury physio," "back pain treatment," "post-surgery rehab," and "physiotherapy for seniors" — all at once.

It ranks for none of them. And the tragedy: when someone lands on it after a "sports injury physiotherapist near me" search, they find a generic list, not a page about *their* torn hamstring. No relevance for Google, no reassurance for the human. This lesson fixes that — with the two page types that generate almost every local lead an SMB will get: **service pages** and **location pages.**

## Why this pays

Service and location pages are the money pages. This is where "physiotherapy in Bengaluru" traffic turns into a phone ringing at the clinic. Optimising a homepage feels productive; building a proper sports-injury page that ranks and books is what actually shows up in the client's calendar. When you sell "page optimisation," these are the pages you mean.

## One page per service beats one vague "Services" page

Google ranks *pages*, not businesses. A page laser-focused on one service — its own title, H1 and body copy answering one intent — is far more relevant to "sports injury physiotherapist Indiranagar" than a page juggling nine services. And it's more persuasive: a runner with a bad knee wants a page about runners' knees, not a menu.

So: **one dedicated page per core service.** For Revive that's `/sports-injury-physiotherapy`, `/back-and-neck-pain`, `/post-surgery-rehabilitation`, and so on. The old "Services" page becomes a hub that describes each and links to the full page.

:::note[🎓 Foundations]
**Why not just one big page with everything?** Because relevance is per-page. Think of each service page as a specialist you send to a specific interview. One generalist mumbling about nine topics loses to five specialists, each nailing their one question. More focused pages = more entry points into the site = more searches you can win.
:::

## A reusable service-page template

Every service page follows the same skeleton. Once you've built one, you're filling in a template — fast, consistent, and complete.

:::note[📋 Swipe this]
**Service-page template:**

1. **H1** — `[Service] in [Locality]` (e.g. *Sports Injury Physiotherapy in Indiranagar*).
2. **The problem** (1 short para) — name the visitor's pain in their words. *"Sharp knee pain every time you run? A niggle that won't settle after that weekend match?"*
3. **The solution / your approach** — how you treat it, in plain language. Hands-on assessment, treatment plan, what recovery looks like.
4. **Proof** — years of experience, number of patients helped, credentials, before/after (with consent), 2–3 short reviews specific to this service.
5. **Service details** — what's included, typical number of sessions, what to expect in the first visit, pricing transparency where sensible.
6. **FAQs** — 4–6 real questions ("How many sessions will I need?" "Do you accept walk-ins?" "Is it covered by insurance?").
7. **CTA block** — call button, WhatsApp, "Book assessment" form. Repeated near the top and bottom.
8. **NAP + embedded map** — clinic Name, Address, Phone and a Google Map, so Google and humans both confirm you're local and real.
:::

Notice the template serves both audiences at once. The H1 and the problem paragraph feed Google relevance *and* make the human feel understood. The proof and FAQs build trust *and* answer the "People also ask" queries. The CTA converts. Nothing on the page is decoration.

## Location pages — done right, not doorway spam

Revive has two branches: Indiranagar and HSR Layout. That justifies a **location page per branch** — genuinely useful pages that help someone in HSR Layout choose the HSR clinic. Done right, a location page is fantastic local SEO. Done wrong, it's a penalty waiting to happen.

**A location page done right** is *unique and useful*. The HSR Layout page has: that branch's real address and map, its actual phone number, its team, its opening hours, parking and metro directions, photos of *that* clinic, reviews from *that* branch's patients, and a line or two about the neighbourhood it serves. A person reading it learns something real about visiting *that* location.

:::caution[⚠️ Gotcha]
**Doorway pages — the location-spam trap that gets sites penalised.** Here's the black-hat move: generate 60 near-identical pages — "Physiotherapy in Koramangala," "Physiotherapy in Whitefield," "Physiotherapy in Jayanagar" — where the *only* difference is the area name swapped in by find-and-replace. You have no clinic in those areas and nothing unique to say about them. Google explicitly targets these "doorway pages," and thin, duplicated location pages are a well-known way to *lose* rankings, not gain them. **Rule: only build a location page for a place you genuinely serve, and make each one materially unique.** No branch there? Don't fake a page for it.
:::

Two real branches → two rich location pages: great. One clinic pretending to have twelve "locations" across the city → doorway spam: avoid.

## Local relevance signals — sprinkle the real stuff

Make each money page unmistakably *local* with details only a real local business would know:

- **NAP** (Name, Address, Phone) consistent with the Google Business Profile — same format, every page.
- **Embedded Google Map** of the actual branch, plus **local landmarks & directions** ("opposite Indiranagar metro station," "5 minutes from CMH Road").
- **Area names** used naturally, not stuffed — the neighbourhoods you actually serve.
- **Real photos** of the clinic and team, not stock images of a gleaming clinic in California.
- **Reviews** from local patients, ideally mentioning the service and area.

These are the fingerprints of a real local business. They reassure the human and help Google connect the page to the place.

## Common mistakes

- **The everything-page.** One "Services" page for nine services. Split it.
- **Fake locations.** Location pages for areas with no branch — doorway spam.
- **Copy-paste location pages.** Same 400 words with the area name swapped. Google notices.
- **CTA hidden at the bottom.** A person ready to book shouldn't have to scroll for the phone number.
- **Stock photos everywhere.** Nothing screams "faceless" louder in a trust business.

Write the H1, the problem paragraph, three FAQs and the CTA. This becomes one of the money pages in your module Assignment.

<summary>Q: Why beat one focused service page over a single "Services" page that lists everything?</summary>

Google ranks pages by relevance to a query, and a page about one service is far more relevant to that service's search than a page splitting attention nine ways. Focused pages also give you more entry points — one per intent — and let you speak directly to that visitor's specific problem, which converts better.

<details>
<summary>Q: Your client has one clinic. They ask for 15 "location" pages covering every Bengaluru neighbourhood to "rank everywhere." What do you tell them?</summary>

No. Pages for areas you don't serve, differing only by a swapped area name, are doorway pages — thin, duplicated, and a known way to get penalised, not ranked. Build location pages only for real branches, and make each genuinely unique. For nearby areas you *serve from* an existing branch, mention them honestly on that branch's page instead.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q: What makes a location page "genuinely useful" rather than spam?</summary>

Unique, real, branch-specific content: that location's address, map, phone, team, hours, photos, reviews, and local directions. If a visitor learns something true and specific about visiting *that* clinic, it's a real location page. If it's the same template with the area name find-and-replaced, it's spam.
</details>
