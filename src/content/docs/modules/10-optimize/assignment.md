This is the module's whole point compressed into one small, powerful artefact. Anyone can *say* "I do data-driven optimisation." You're going to have the receipt: a log that shows you read the scoreboard, formed a hypothesis, changed one thing, and tracked what happened. Three times over, minimum. It's the difference between a freelancer who guesses and one who improves — written down, dated, undeniable.

## The brief

Produce an **optimisation log** for your capstone client documenting **three or more data-driven changes**. Each entry must trace a real number to a specific change to a checkable outcome. No "I felt like the homepage needed work." Every change starts in the data and ends with something you'll measure.

## Steps / checklist

1. **Review the data.** Open GA4, Search Console, and (if the client is running them) Google Ads. Read the four GA4 reports that matter, skim GSC for high-impression/low-CTR pages and striking-distance queries, and — if Ads are live — check the search terms report.
2. **Identify 3+ specific opportunities.** Each must come *from the data*, not from a hunch. Examples:
   - A page with strong impressions but weak CTR → title rewrite.
   - A query ranking position 5–15 → improve/interlink the page.
   - Search terms wasting spend → add negative keywords.
   - A high-traffic page with a poor conversion rate → fix the landing page.
   - A stale Google Business Profile → fresh Posts / fix categories / drive reviews.
3. **Make each change** — one variable per change, so the result is attributable. Note the date.
4. **Record each entry** in the log table below: what you observed (with the number), what you changed, your hypothesis, and the result you'll check (plus the date you'll check it).
5. **Close the loop where you can.** If a change is old enough to have an early result, record it. If it's too fresh, note the check-back date — an open, honest "TBD on [date]" is a valid entry.

:::caution[⚠️ Gotcha]
**One variable per change, or the log is worthless.** If an entry says "rewrote the title, added two internal links, and changed the hero image," you'll never know which move did anything. Split it into separate entries, or pick the single change you can actually measure. The one-variable discipline *is* the skill being graded here.
:::

## Deliverable format

A simple **optimisation log** — a spreadsheet, a table in a doc, or a Notion table. Client-facing quality, but it doesn't need to be pretty; it needs to be honest and repeatable. One row per change:

| Date | Observation (the data) | Change made | Hypothesis | Result to check (+ when) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| e.g. 2026-08-03 | "Treatments" page: 2,400 impressions, 0.9% CTR (GSC) | Rewrote title to "Painless Root Canal in Jayanagar — Meena Dental" | Benefit + area in title lifts CTR toward ~3% | CTR in GSC on 2026-08-24 |
| e.g. 2026-08-05 | Search terms show ₹600 spent on "dental jobs" (Ads) | Added "jobs", "vacancy", "salary" as negative keywords | Cuts wasted spend, lifts lead-relevant CTR | Wasted-spend terms + CPL next week |
| e.g. 2026-08-08 | Home page 70% mobile, mobile conv. rate 0.6% (GA4) | Moved click-to-call button above the fold on mobile | Easier call access lifts mobile call taps | Mobile key events on 2026-08-22 |

*(The rows above are illustrative composites — replace with your client's real data.)*

Keep it living. This isn't a one-time hand-in; it's the document you'll add to every week for as long as you have the client.

## Self-assessment rubric

Grade yourself honestly. "Client-ready" means you'd defend every row to a sceptical owner.

| Criterion | Missing | Getting there | Client-ready |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Data actually read** | Changes are hunches, no numbers cited | Some entries reference data, some don't | Every entry starts with a specific number from GA4/GSC/Ads |
| **Changes justified by data** | Change doesn't follow from the observation | Loose connection between data and change | Each change is the logical response to its observation, with a clear hypothesis |
| **One-variable discipline** | Multiple changes lumped per entry | Mostly one change, some bundled | Exactly one variable per entry, so results are attributable |
| **Results tracked** | No result column, no check-backs | Result-to-check named but no dates | Every entry has a metric *and* a date to check it, with early results recorded where available |

If any row sits in "Missing," go back to the relevant lesson before calling the module done. A log full of unjustified, multi-change, un-tracked entries isn't proof you optimise — it's proof you don't.

## How this feeds your capstone

When you hand over the capstone report in [Module 11](/seo-sem-analytics-playbook/modules/11-report/), this log is your evidence that the results weren't luck. Anyone can show a chart that went up. You can show *why* it went up — the deliberate, documented, one-variable-at-a-time decisions behind the line. That's the story that turns a three-month project into a two-year retainer: you don't guess, you improve, and you can prove it monthly.
