You run a quick audit on Meena's Dental Clinic and find her phone number listed four different ways. The Google profile has her new number. JustDial has the old landline. Practo has a number that now rings a stranger. Her own website footer has a third mobile with a typo. Google looks at this mess and thinks: *are these even the same business? Which number is real? Can I trust any of this?* And it quietly trusts her a little less.

That's the whole lesson in one scene. **NAP consistency** — Name, Address, Phone identical everywhere on the web — is the boring, unglamorous plumbing of local SEO. It won't win you a "wow" from the client. But inconsistent NAP silently drags down prominence and confuses customers, and fixing it is one of the highest-return chores you'll do.

## What NAP is and why Google cares

**NAP = Name, Address, Phone number.** Every time these three appear together on the web — on your Google profile, on JustDial, on your website, in a local directory — that's a **citation**. Google reads citations as votes of existence and consistency: the more places that agree on the *exact same* Name, Address and Phone, the more confident Google is that the business is real, established and trustworthy. That confidence feeds **prominence**, one of the three ranking pillars from the last-but-one lesson.

The keyword is *identical*. Not "close." Not "basically the same." Identical.

:::note[🎓 Foundations]
A **citation** is any mention of a business's name, address and phone number online — with or without a link. A directory listing (JustDial, Sulekha), a mention in a local blog, an entry on a maps service: all citations. They're one of the classic prominence signals in local SEO, which is why getting them right and consistent matters.
:::

## Consistency beats volume — every time

New freelancers get this backwards. They think local SEO is a numbers game: blast the business onto 200 directories, more is more. Wrong. **Fifty perfectly consistent citations beat two hundred sloppy ones.** A pile of listings that disagree with each other actively *hurts* — each inconsistency is a little "hmm, are these the same business?" for Google and a little "which number do I call?" for the customer.

So the mindset is: pick the citations that matter, get every field byte-for-byte identical, and keep them that way. Quality and consistency over raw count.

### The devil is in these details

Decide the *canonical* format once — matching the Google Business Profile exactly — and use it everywhere:

- **Name:** exactly as on the profile and signage. "Meena's Dental Clinic," not "Meena Dental Clinic," not "Dr. Meena's Dental Care."
- **Address:** same abbreviations, same order, same spelling. Pick "Rd" or "Road" and stick with it. Same floor/landmark wording. Same 6-digit PIN code, every time.
- **Phone:** one primary number, one format. Decide on "+91 98xxx xxxxx" (or whichever) and never mix landline-here, mobile-there.

:::caution[⚠️ Gotcha]
**Three different phone numbers across listings is a classic prominence killer** — and a real revenue leak, because a customer who calls a dead number just calls a competitor instead. This happens constantly when a business changes SIMs, adds a call-tracking number, or lists a receptionist's personal mobile "temporarily." Standardise on ONE primary number, fix it everywhere, and treat every future number change as a project (update all citations), not a shrug.
:::

## Auditing existing citations

Before you build anything, find out what's already out there — the good, the wrong and the duplicated.

1. **Search the business name** in Google and Google Maps. Note every listing that appears.
2. **Search the phone number** (each variant you can find) in quotes — this surfaces listings you didn't know existed and reveals which wrong numbers are floating around.
3. **Search the address.** Same idea — catch listings with old or mistyped addresses.
4. **Check the big India directories directly** (list below) for existing entries.
5. **Log everything in a simple sheet:** columns for Site, URL, Name-listed, Address-listed, Phone-listed, and a Status (Correct / Wrong / Duplicate / Missing).

## Building and cleaning citations for Indian SMBs

Now fix the wrong ones (claim the listing and edit, or contact the directory) and add the missing important ones. For Indian local businesses, these are the citations that actually pull weight:

**India citation-sites checklist** (prioritise the top ones; add industry-specific ones as relevant):

- [ ] **Google Business Profile** — the anchor; everything must match *this*
- [ ] **Bing Places** — the easy second search engine listing
- [ ] **Apple Maps / Apple Business Connect** — matters for iPhone users' "near me"
- [ ] **JustDial** — huge in India, drives real enquiry calls
- [ ] **Sulekha** — strong for services (home, professional, tutors)
- [ ] **IndiaMART** — essential for B2B, manufacturers, wholesalers, suppliers
- [ ] **The client's own website** — footer + a Contact page + LocalBusiness structured data
- [ ] **Social profiles** — Facebook Page, Instagram (NAP in bio/about)
- [ ] **Industry-specific directories** — Practo/Lybrate (clinics), Zomato/Swiggy (restaurants), UrbanClap/Urban Company (home services), MagicBricks/99acres (real estate), Shiksha/UrbanPro (coaching/tutors)
- [ ] **Local/city directories & chambers** relevant to the town

Get the Name, Address and Phone **identical** on every single one. That consistency is the whole point — a shorter list done perfectly beats a long list done carelessly.

:::caution[⚠️ Gotcha]
JustDial and similar aggressive-sales directories sometimes **create or edit listings themselves** and may push a call-tracking number or a slightly different name. Claim the listing so *you* control the fields, and re-check it periodically — these can drift back out of sync and quietly break your NAP consistency without anyone touching it.
:::

## Common mistakes

- Chasing citation *count* while ignoring consistency (the exact wrong priority).
- Letting a number/address change touch only Google and nowhere else.
- Never claiming the JustDial/directory listing, so it drifts out of sync.
- Two spellings of the same street, or "Rd" here and "Road" there.
- Duplicate listings on the same directory — pick one, remove/merge the rest.

## Do this now

Run the four-search audit (name, phone, address, plus the big directories) on your capstone client and log every listing in a sheet with a Status column. Standardise the canonical NAP format from the Google profile. Fix the **worst inconsistency today** — usually a wrong phone number that's actively losing calls.
