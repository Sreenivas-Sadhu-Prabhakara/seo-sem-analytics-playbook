A fully optimised profile is a car with a full tank. Posts, Q&A, products and messaging are actually *driving* it. This is where a static "we exist" listing becomes a living shopfront that publishes offers, answers questions before they're asked, and turns a search into a WhatsApp message or a booking. It's also — conveniently for you — the part that's naturally **ongoing**, which is exactly what a monthly retainer is made of.

Set-and-forget profiles slide down the rankings over time; active ones hold and climb. Google favours businesses that behave like they're open, engaged and paying attention. So let's give it that.

## Google Posts: your free megaphone

**Posts** are short updates that show on your profile in Search and Maps — offers, news, events, new services. They're free, they signal an active business, and they give a searcher a reason to tap *now*.

Types you'll use most:

- **Offer** — "20% off teeth cleaning this month" with dates and a coupon feel.
- **Update** — general news: a new dentist joined, extended weekend hours, a festival greeting.
- **Event** — a free dental check-up camp on Sunday, a workshop, a sale weekend.

Each post wants a **clear photo, a short punchy line, and a call-to-action button** (Call, Book, Learn more, Order). Keep the copy tight — this is a billboard, not a blog.

:::caution[⚠️ Gotcha]
Some post types (especially offers/updates) **expire** after about a week if they're not evergreen, and an empty, stale posts section looks worse than a modest active one. Don't post once, feel accomplished, and vanish for three months. Cadence beats intensity: a steady rhythm keeps the profile looking alive. Also — no phone numbers or spammy keyword-stuffing in posts; write for the human who'll read it.
:::

## Q&A: answer the questions before they're asked

The **Questions & answers** section on the profile is public, and here's the catch most owners miss: **anyone can answer**, including random strangers and competitors. An unmonitored Q&A is a liability; a managed one is a conversion tool.

Two jobs:

1. **Seed the common questions yourself.** As the business, post-and-answer the real FAQs: "Do you accept UPI/insurance?" "Is parking available?" "Do you do root canals in one sitting?" "Are you open on Sundays?" This is legitimate — you're providing genuinely useful info — and it fills the section with correct answers instead of leaving it to chance.
2. **Monitor for spam and wrong answers.** Owners should get notified of new questions and answer fast, and watch for a competitor posting a misleading "answer." Helpful answers can be upvoted so they surface first.

:::tip[🚀 Level up]
**Use Q&A to pre-empt objections.** Think like a salesperson: what makes a customer hesitate right before they call? Price anxiety, "will it hurt," "do they take my payment method," "is it far/parking." Seed a calm, reassuring Q&A for each: "Is a root canal painful? — Modern RCT is done under local anaesthesia and most patients feel little to no pain; we'll walk you through every step." You're not just informing — you're removing the last friction between search and booking, right there on the profile.
:::

## Products & Services listings: the visual menu

The **Products/Services** section lets you showcase specific offerings with a photo, name, price (or price range) and description — a mini visual catalogue on the profile itself. A restaurant shows signature dishes; a salon shows packages; a boutique shows collections; a clinic lists treatments. It adds relevant, legitimate keywords *and* gives the customer something concrete to react to before they've even called.

Keep entries real and current — don't list a service the business doesn't offer, and update or remove seasonal ones. For prices, a range or "starting at" is fine where exact prices vary.

## Messaging and the WhatsApp reality

The profile can offer **messaging** so searchers text the business directly from Search/Maps, and it surfaces basic performance data on enquiries. Useful — *but* here's the ground truth in India:

**Many owners live on WhatsApp, not the Google messaging inbox.** They'll miss messages in a channel they don't check, and a missed enquiry is a lost customer — worse than no messaging at all. So be pragmatic:

- If the owner will genuinely monitor Google messaging (or it routes somewhere they watch), enable it and set expectations for reply speed.
- If not, **don't turn on a channel nobody answers.** Instead, use the profile to drive customers into the channels the business *does* watch — a prominent **Call** button, a **website/booking link**, and where appropriate a **WhatsApp click-to-chat** link (`wa.me/<number>`) surfaced through the website or a post's call-to-action.
- Lean into how Indian SMBs actually transact: a tap that becomes a **call**, a **WhatsApp enquiry**, or a **UPI-enabled booking**. Meet customers where they are.

:::caution[⚠️ Gotcha]
Enabling messaging and then leaving it unanswered is worse than not offering it — Google shows a typical response time, and "responds in a few days" (or a wall of ignored messages) signals a business that doesn't care. Only switch on a channel someone will actually watch. An unwatched inbox is a promise you break in public.
:::

## Read the performance insights

The profile's **performance/insights** view is your reporting goldmine — it shows what people did *because of* the listing:

- **Calls** — taps on the call button.
- **Direction requests** — people navigating to the shop (a great proxy for real footfall/intent).
- **Website clicks** and **bookings**.
- **Searches** — the terms people used to find the profile (great for spotting new services to add or post about).

This is how you *prove your worth*. "Calls from your Google profile went from 20 to 65 a month" is a sentence that renews retainers. Screenshot the trend, translate it into the owner's language (calls, visits, customers — not "impressions"), and you've turned invisible SEO work into a felt result. ## Common mistakes

- Posting once, then silence — a stale posts section reads as a dead business.
- Leaving Q&A unmonitored (competitors and spammers fill the void).
- Enabling messaging nobody checks (broken promise, visible slow response time).
- Reporting "impressions" to an owner who only cares about calls and walk-ins.
- Listing products/services the business doesn't actually offer.
