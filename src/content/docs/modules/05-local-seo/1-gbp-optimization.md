You open Meena's Dental Clinic on Google Maps and your heart sinks a little. The category says "Doctor." There are two photos, both blurry, both from 2019. The hours are blank, so Google quietly shows "Hours might differ." There's no description, no services listed, and the phone number is the owner's personal mobile from before she switched SIMs. This profile isn't ranking because it's barely *finished*.

Good news: this is the most fixable problem in local SEO, and fixing it is where you earn your first visible win. **Complete profiles rank; half-built ones don't.** Google rewards businesses that give it clean, rich, trustworthy information, because that's what it wants to show searchers. Your job this lesson is to take a profile from "barely there" to "so complete Google has no excuse."

## Step 0: Claim and verify — the gate to everything

You can't optimise what you don't control. If the profile exists but nobody manages it, you **claim** it; if it doesn't exist, you **create** it. Either way you must **verify** ownership — this is the gate, and until you're through it you can change nothing.

Verification methods vary by business, and Google decides which you get — you don't:

- **Video verification** — increasingly the default. You record a short unedited video showing the storefront/signage, the surrounding area, and some proof you run the place (keys, equipment, back office). Follow the on-screen prompts.
- **Phone or SMS** — Google calls or texts a code. Fast when offered.
- **Email** — a code to the business email, when eligible.
- **Postcard** — the old-school mailer with a code to the physical address, taking a week or more. Rarer now but still appears.

:::caution[⚠️ Gotcha]
Verification can be finicky, and India addresses with informal landmarks ("opposite Reliance Fresh, above the medical store") trip it up. Enter the address exactly as it appears on official signage and utility bills, keep the storefront board visible in any video, and don't create a second duplicate listing out of frustration — duplicates get the whole profile suspended. If verification fails, request it again; don't rage-click.
:::

## The business name: real name only

Say this out loud so it sticks: **the business name field is for the business's real-world name. Nothing else.** Not "Meena's Dental Clinic — Best Dentist in Jayanagar | Painless RCT | Braces." That's keyword stuffing, it's an explicit violation of Google's guidelines, and it is one of the fastest ways to get a profile **suspended**. It also invites competitors to report you, which they will.

The name on the profile should match the name on the board outside the shop. That's the rule, and it's not negotiable.

:::caution[⚠️ Gotcha]
Yes, stuffed names sometimes rank — until they don't. A suspension can wipe the profile's reviews and history and take weeks of appeals to recover, if it recovers at all. You never want to explain to a client why their five years of reviews vanished because you crammed keywords into the name to chase a short-term bump. Win with categories and prominence instead. Those are legitimate levers, and they're stronger.
:::

## Categories: the single biggest ranking lever you control

If you take one thing from this lesson, take this: **your primary category is the heaviest thing you can put on the relevance scale.** It tells Google what the business fundamentally *is*, and it decides which searches you're even eligible to appear for.

- **Primary category** — the one that best describes the core business. For Meena, "Dental clinic" or "Dentist," not "Doctor." Precision matters: "Dentist" and "Dental clinic" and "Cosmetic dentist" are different, and the right one depends on what the business mainly does and what people search.
- **Secondary categories** — add the genuinely relevant ones (e.g. "Orthodontist," "Dental implants provider" if those are real services). Don't spray unrelated categories to catch more searches; irrelevant categories dilute relevance and can look spammy.

How to choose well: look at what your top-ranking local competitors use as their primary category (you can often see a business's category on its profile), and match the search language your customers actually use. Then pick the most specific accurate category, not the broadest.

## Fill in everything else — completeness is the whole game

Now the grind that pays. Work down the profile and leave nothing blank:

1. **Services / products.** List the actual services with short, honest descriptions — "Root canal treatment," "Teeth cleaning & polishing," "Braces & aligners." This adds relevant keywords *legitimately*, in the field built for them.
2. **Attributes.** Tick the true ones — "Wheelchair accessible," "Online appointments," "Accepts UPI/cards," "Women-led," "Free parking." These power filters and build trust.
3. **Hours — including holiday hours.** Set regular hours accurately, and *always* set **special hours** for Diwali, Independence Day, local bandhs and the like. A customer who drives over on a holiday to a "closed" shop marked "open" leaves a one-star review. Google also nudges profiles that keep hours current.
4. **Service areas** (for businesses that go to the customer — plumbers, home tutors, caterers). List the localities you actually serve. A storefront that customers visit should show its address, not hide behind a wide service area.
5. **Phone & website.** One consistent primary number (we'll obsess over this in the NAP lesson). Use a real, working number — ideally one that can take calls *and* WhatsApp.
6. **Description.** Write a natural, keyword-aware paragraph about the business — what it does, who it serves, the area. Weave in terms people search ("family dental clinic in Jayanagar," "painless root canal") but write for a human, not a bot. No stuffing, no ALL CAPS, no phone numbers or URLs (they get stripped).

## Photos: fresh, real, and plentiful

Photos are not decoration — they're a ranking and conversion signal, and they're the first thing a customer judges. Cover the full set:

- **Exterior** — the storefront and signage, so people recognise it from the road.
- **Interior** — clean, well-lit, welcoming. Waiting area, chair, reception.
- **Team** — real faces build trust; a dentist people can picture is a dentist people book.
- **Work / products** — before/afters (with consent), the food, the finished haircut, the gym floor.

Add photos **regularly**, not once. A profile that gets fresh images every few weeks signals an active, real business. Encourage the owner to shoot a few decent phone photos monthly — that becomes part of your retainer's easy wins.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the name field as ad space (suspension bait).
- Picking a broad category ("Doctor") over the precise one ("Dentist").
- Leaving hours or services blank because "the website has them."
- Two blurry photos and calling it done.
- Creating a duplicate listing instead of claiming the existing one.

## Do this now

Open your capstone client's profile (or create one if it doesn't exist) and run it against the completeness checklist above. Fix the **three biggest gaps today** — almost always: primary category, hours, and photos. Screenshot the "before" first; you'll want it for the Assignment.
