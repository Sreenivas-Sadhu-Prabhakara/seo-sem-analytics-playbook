This is the assignment the whole course hinges on. Everything after this module — local SEO, GA4, Google Ads, reporting — you'll do *for a real business.* So your job now is to go get one.

Not a hypothetical. Not "I'll find one later." An actual capstone client who has said yes.

## The brief

Recruit **one real capstone client** — a local SMB in your niche who agrees to let you work on their online presence — and deliver them a **filled one-page proposal** with an agreed scope. Paid, discounted, or a case-study freebie all count. The *yes* is what matters; the rate is negotiable.

A friend's, relative's, or neighbour's business at a "case-study rate" is not cheating — it's the smart, standard way to land client #1. Warm, low-pressure, real. Use it.

## Steps / checklist

Work straight down the module you just finished:

- [ ] **Shortlist** from your recon: take your prospect list (Lesson 2) and pick your **top 1–3 candidates** — weighting warm intros and visible, fixable problems.
- [ ] **Pre-audit** the top candidate: run the full 15-minute pre-audit (Lesson 3) and fill in the checklist. Note the single most revenue-relevant finding.
- [ ] **Reach out** with the specific insight (Lesson 6): use the observation → cost → curiosity opener, through the warmest channel available. Lead with free value, not a pitch.
- [ ] **Send the one-page proposal** (Lesson 5): their problem, your plan, deliverables, timeline, price (from your tiers in Lesson 4), and one clear next step.
- [ ] **Agree scope** (Lesson 5): lock down what's included, what's *not*, payment terms, and cancellation — even an informal written "I agree" on WhatsApp beats a handshake.
- [ ] **Record the yes:** who agreed, what scope, what rate (even if ₹0 case-study).

:::note[💰 Rupee check]
Illustrative and encouraged: for client #1, a **free or steeply discounted case-study rate in exchange for a testimonial and permission to document results** is a perfectly professional choice — as long as it's *one* client, deliberate, time-boxed, and backed by a written scope so it doesn't drift into free-forever. From client #2, price properly using your Lesson 4 tiers.
:::

## Deliverable format

Two things, saved where you can find them:

1. **The filled one-page proposal** (using the Lesson 5 template) — real business, real pre-audit finding, real plan, real price.
2. **A short note recording the outcome:** who agreed, the scope you settled on (what's in, what's out, payment terms), and the rate. If they've signed/agreed, note how (signed PDF, WhatsApp confirmation, verbal-then-followed-up-in-writing).

## Simulated Client fallback (clearly hypothetical)

No real client this week? Don't stall the course — adopt this **fictional but realistic** capstone client and do every future module for *them*. Swap in a real one the moment you land one.

> **GreenLeaf Dental (hypothetical composite — not a real clinic).** A 2-chair dental practice in Indiranagar, Bengaluru, run by Dr. Nair, open six years, mostly walk-ins and word of mouth. Their **Google Business Profile is unclaimed**, listed under a vague category, with **11 reviews (4.1★) and no owner replies** — including one unanswered complaint near the top. They have a **basic single-page website** that loads slowly on mobile and hides the phone number below the fold. **They run no Google Ads**, while two nearby clinics do and sit above them in the map pack for "dentist in Indiranagar." Dr. Nair is friendly, busy, budget-conscious, and has never worked with a marketer. A new patient's first treatment is worth a few thousand rupees, and many stay for years.

Run the pre-audit on GreenLeaf, draft the opener, fill the proposal, and settle a scope — exactly as you would for a real client. GreenLeaf then becomes your capstone throughout the course.

## Self-assessment rubric

Grade yourself honestly against each criterion:

| Criterion | Missing | Getting there | Client-ready |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prospected properly** | Picked a business at random, no qualification | Shortlisted, but weak on fit/ability-to-pay | Shortlisted by visible problems + ability to pay + warm access |
| **Specific insight in outreach** | Generic "I do digital marketing" | One vague observation, some jargon | A specific, true, jargon-free finding framed as observation → cost → curiosity |
| **Clear proposal** | No proposal, or a bloated deck | One page but fuzzy plan/price | One page: their problem, plan, deliverables, timeline, price, next step — all plain |
| **Scope agreed** | Handshake only, nothing written | Verbal agreement, no exclusions stated | Written scope: included, *not* included, payment terms, cancellation |

Anything not yet "Client-ready"? Fix that column before you call the assignment done. This document is going in front of a real business.

## How this feeds your capstone

The business you just landed is **your client for every module ahead.** In Module 2 you'll recon them properly; later you'll optimise their Google Business Profile, build their local SEO, wire up GA4 to measure it, and run Google Ads to accelerate it — then report the results back to *this owner.* You're not doing exercises anymore. You have a client, a scope, and a reason to get everything right. That's exactly where a freelancer should be standing at the end of week one.
