Your capstone client — let's say it's Meena's Dental Clinic in Jayanagar, a composite of every earnest small-clinic owner you'll ever meet — leans across the desk and says: "My cousin's friend does Google ads for ₹500. Can we not just do that?"

This is the moment. You can either take her money and run a campaign that quietly bleeds, or you can be the one professional in her life who asks the unfashionable question first: *should you advertise at all, and on what?* Sometimes the honest answer is "not yet," and saying so is exactly why she'll trust you with the bigger budget later.

## Why this decision pays

Ads is the only lever in this whole course where being *wrong* costs the client cash daily, not just opportunity. Get the go/no-go call right and you look like a genius who saved them from themselves. Get it wrong and you're the freelancer who lit ₹20,000 on fire chasing clicks for a service with no margin. Your judgement here — before any campaign is built — is the most valuable thing you sell.

## What ads actually are: rented traffic

SEO is *owning* a shop on the high street. It takes months to build, but once you rank, the visits keep coming whether or not you're paying that day. Google Ads is *renting a stall at the market*. The instant you pay, you're front and centre. The instant you stop, you vanish. No equity accrues.

That's not a criticism — rented traffic has three superpowers organic can't match:

- **Instant.** Live in hours, not months. Perfect for a business with zero rankings.
- **Controllable.** You choose the exact search, the city, the time of day, the budget.
- **Measurable.** With tracking wired, you know that ₹3,000 produced 11 calls. Try getting that clarity from a JustDial listing.

The catch is in the word *rented*. Stop paying, traffic stops. So ads is a tool for *now* — cash flow, launches, tests — while SEO builds the thing you'll own later.

:::note[🎓 Foundations]
"Intent" is the whole game in search. Someone typing **"emergency dentist Jayanagar Sunday"** has their wallet half-open. Someone typing **"does flossing actually work"** is killing time. Ads pay off when you buy the first kind of search and ignore the second. High intent = someone ready to act, not someone reading.
:::

## Good fits: when to reach for ads

Advertise when the maths and the intent line up:

- **New business, no rankings yet.** A gym that opened last month can't wait six months for SEO. Ads buy visibility today.
- **High-intent, high-margin services.** Root canals, laser eye surgery, packers-and-movers, coaching-class admissions. One conversion pays for many clicks.
- **Promotions and time-boxed offers.** "Diwali 30% off," "monsoon AC service." Ads flip on for the window and off after.
- **Seasonal peaks.** Wedding photographers in wedding season; tax consultants in July.
- **Fast keyword testing.** Not sure whether people search "dental implants" or "teeth fixing cost"? Ads tell you which converts in a week — data you can then feed into your SEO plan.

## Poor fits: when to say no (or wait)

- **Near-zero margin.** If the product nets ₹40 and a click costs ₹25, you need an impossible conversion rate to survive. Walk away.
- **A tiny budget spread too thin.** ₹100/day across a whole city and ten keywords buys nothing but heartbreak. Concentrate or don't bother.
- **Purely informational intent.** Advertising against "what is GST" for a CA firm burns money on people who want a blog, not a bill.
- **No landing page and no way to answer enquiries.** If clicks land on a broken site or calls go unanswered, you're paying to disappoint people.

:::caution[⚠️ Gotcha]
The single most common way freelancers destroy campaigns: **launching with no conversion tracking.** Without it, Google's smart bidding is flying blind, and *you* can't tell a call-generating keyword from a money-pit. You'll optimise toward clicks — the vanity metric — instead of leads. Never, ever launch a campaign whose success you can't measure. We wire tracking in [lesson 6](/seo-sem-analytics-playbook/modules/09-google-ads/6-landing-pages-and-conversion/); until it exists, the campaign stays in draft.
:::

## Ads and SEO are teammates, not rivals

The pro move is to run both. Ads buy the top-of-page visibility *now* while your SEO work matures over the next few months. Better still, the two feed each other: your search-terms report from ads reveals the exact phrases real customers type, which you hand straight to your on-page and content work. And when a keyword gets expensive or you finally rank organically for it, you can dial the ad spend down. Rented traffic funds the wait for owned traffic.

## Auction basics in one breath

Every time someone searches, Google runs a lightning-fast auction. You don't simply pay the most and win. Your position is set by **Ad Rank ≈ your bid × Quality Score** (roughly: how relevant and useful your ad and landing page are) plus the impact of your assets. That's genuinely good news for the little guy: a tightly relevant ₹15 bid can outrank a lazy ₹40 bid. Relevance is a discount. We'll use that fact ruthlessly.

:::note[💰 Rupee check]
*Illustrative only — the market moves, so verify current rates in your niche.* A **minimum viable** local Search budget is usually in the ballpark of **₹300–₹800/day** for a single tight ad group in one city. Below roughly ₹200/day you often can't gather enough data to learn anything. Cost-per-click swings wildly by niche — a few rupees for a low-competition local service, ₹80+ for cutthroat categories like insurance, loans, or "packers and movers." Always quote the client a *range* and say plainly that CPCs are set by a live auction, not by you.
:::

:::tip[⚔️ War story]
A freelancer (composite) took ₹8,000/month from a home-tuition tutor and split it across "education," "tuition," "learning," and "study" — broad, vague, city-wide. Clicks poured in from students looking for free notes and parents in other states. Zero enquiries in three weeks. The problem wasn't the budget; it was advertising against low-intent, untargeted searches. Re-pointed at **"home tuition maths class 10 [locality]"** with a tight geo, the same money produced real enquiries. Intent and focus beat spend every time.
:::

## Common mistakes

- Advertising because a competitor does, without checking if the maths works for *this* business.
- Bidding on broad, informational terms instead of high-intent, ready-to-buy searches.
- Launching before conversion tracking exists — optimising blind.
- Spreading a small budget so thin it never learns anything.

## Do this now

For your capstone client, write **three sentences**: (1) the single service most worth advertising, (2) one specific high-intent search a ready customer would type for it, and (3) an honest go/no-go call on their margin and budget. If it's a "no," write why — that clarity is a deliverable too.

<details>
<summary>Q: A sweet shop nets ₹15 profit on a ₹60 box of ladoos and wants to advertise "sweets near me" where clicks average ₹18. What do you tell them?</summary>

Don't run it as-is — the margin can't support the click cost, so you'd need an unrealistic conversion rate just to break even. Redirect the budget toward higher-value orders (bulk/festival/corporate gifting) where one conversion covers many clicks, or skip ads and lean on local SEO and Google Business Profile instead.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q: Why is launching a campaign with no conversion tracking almost always a mistake?</summary>

Because you and Google's bidding are then blind to what actually matters — leads. You'll end up optimising toward clicks, the vanity metric, unable to tell a call-generating keyword from a budget drain. Tracking first, spend second.
