The cheapest client you will ever get is the one you already have.

Meena already trusts you. She's already paying you. She's already answering your WhatsApps. Landing her cost you weeks of prospecting and a nervous pitch. Landing the *next thing* she buys costs you one honest sentence at the end of a monthly report. And yet most freelancers grind themselves to dust chasing brand-new strangers while sitting on a warm client who'd happily pay for two more services — if only someone offered.

This lesson is about two levers that quietly double a freelancer's income without doubling their client count: **upsells** (selling more to existing clients) and **productised services** (packaging your work so it's stupidly easy to buy and deliver).

## Upsells: the natural next yes

An upsell isn't a con. It's noticing a real gap in the client's business and offering to fill it. You're already inside Meena's numbers every month; you can see exactly what she's missing. The best upsells feel *obvious* — "of course we should do that next" — because they solve a problem the client can already feel.

The classic upsell ladder for a local SMB:

- **Add Google Ads to an SEO client.** SEO is a slow cooker; Ads is a microwave. When Meena wants patients *this month* — a new branch launch, a slow season, a new implant service — Ads buys instant visibility while SEO keeps compounding. This is the single most common and most valuable upsell, because it's a whole new service line (and a whole new fee).
- **Add content.** One blog post a month becomes four. "Answer the questions your patients actually ask" is an easy sell and directly feeds rankings.
- **Add a new location or service.** She opens a second clinic, or launches Invisalign. Each is a fresh Profile, fresh pages, fresh keywords — new scope, new money.
- **Website rebuild or refresh.** You keep tripping over a slow, ugly, un-trackable site? That's not a complaint; that's a project. (You don't have to build it yourself — you can project-manage a developer and mark it up.)
- **Review-management add-on.** Actively requesting, monitoring and responding to reviews is ongoing work worth its own line item, especially for reputation-sensitive businesses like clinics and restaurants.

:::note[🎓 Foundations]
**Upsell vs. cross-sell, without the jargon:** an *upsell* is more of what solves the client's next problem (adding Ads, adding content). A *cross-sell* is a related service (say, basic reputation management). For a solo freelancer the distinction barely matters — both mean "grow the value of an account you already have." What matters is that it's a *genuine* fit, not a random add-on to pad the invoice.
:::

:::caution[⚠️ Gotcha]
Don't upsell from thin air. Upsell from **evidence**. "Your report shows people search 'root canal near me' a lot but you don't rank for it — a dedicated page plus a small Ads test would catch that demand" lands. "Hey, want to also buy Instagram management?" (a service you can't actually do well) destroys the trust you spent months building. Only sell what you can deliver, and tie every upsell to something visible in their own data.
:::

## Productised services: stop quoting from scratch

Here's the tax nobody warns you about: every custom proposal you write is unpaid work. You scope it, you price it, you second-guess the number, you send it, you wait, you follow up. Do that fifteen times and you've lost a week to selling instead of doing.

A **productised service** kills that tax. It's a fixed-scope, fixed-price offer with a clear name, a clear deliverable, and a clear price — the same every time. You sell it off a menu instead of reinventing it. The client knows exactly what they get; you know exactly what you deliver; and because it's repeatable, you get faster and more profitable at it every time you run it.

Think of the difference between a restaurant with a menu and a restaurant where you have to describe a dish from scratch and negotiate the price every visit. Menus exist because they make buying frictionless. Give your services a menu.

:::note[📋 Swipe this]
**A starter productised service menu (rename and re-price for your market):**

| Package | Fixed scope | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **GBP Launch Pack** | Full Google Business Profile setup/claim, categories, services, hours, 10 photos, first 3 posts, review-request flow | A business invisible on Maps |
| **5-Page SEO Refresh** | On-page optimisation of 5 key pages: titles, meta, headings, internal links, one FAQ/schema block each | An existing site that ranks poorly |
| **Ads Setup Sprint** | One Search campaign built end-to-end: keywords, ad groups, 3 ad variants, negatives, conversion tracking, 2-week babysitting | A client who wants leads *now* |
| **Local Content Kit** | 4 locally-optimised pages/posts (a "near me" landing page + 3 question-answer articles) | Thin sites that need topical depth |
| **Review Rescue** | Review audit, response templates, a review-request system, 30 days of active prompting | A business with few/old/negative reviews |

Each has one price, one timeline, one deliverable checklist. Sell it in a sentence.
:::

The beauty of productised offers is that they're brilliant **on-ramps**. A stranger who won't commit to a retainer will happily buy a ₹-fixed "GBP Launch Pack" — a small, defined, low-risk yes. You over-deliver, they see the results, and *that's* your natural bridge into the monthly retainer from the last lesson. Product first, relationship second, retainer third.

:::tip[🚀 Level up]
**Tiered retainers — good / better / best.** Once you've got a couple of retainer clients, package the retainer itself into three tiers instead of one custom quote. A **Local** tier (Profile upkeep + reviews + report), a **Growth** tier (adds content + on-page work), and a **Growth + Ads** tier (adds full Ads management). Three named tiers do two magic things: they let the client choose *how much* rather than *whether* (the answer shifts from yes/no to which-one), and the middle tier becomes the obvious "sensible" pick — most people avoid the cheapest and the priciest. You anchor high, sell the middle, and never haggle line-by-line again.
:::

**Illustrative only — verify current rates.** Productised packs are typically priced as one-time fixed fees scaled to effort: a small GBP setup pack sits at the lower end, a multi-page SEO refresh or an Ads setup sprint higher, a website-related project higher still. The point of a fixed price isn't to be the cheapest — it's to be *predictable*, so the client buys without a negotiation and you profit more each time you run the same play. Price for the value and your improving speed, not the hours of your first nervous attempt.

## Common mistakes

- **Never asking.** The client can't buy what you don't offer. Most upsells are lost to silence, not rejection.
- **Upselling things you can't deliver** (social media, video, "web design" you've never done). One bad add-on can sink the whole relationship.
- **Endless custom quotes.** If you're re-scoping the same job for the tenth time, productise it.
- **Leading with the retainer to a cold stranger.** A small productised pack is a far easier first yes; the retainer follows the results.

## Do this now

Write your own five-item service menu — steal the Swipe-this table and rewrite each package in your own words, with your own fixed price (use the Rupee check to sanity-check). Then look at your capstone client's latest report and identify **one honest upsell** their data justifies. Draft the single sentence you'd use to offer it.

<details>
<summary>Q: What makes a productised service easier to sell than a custom quote?</summary>

Fixed scope, fixed price, fixed deliverable, and a clear name mean the client knows exactly what they get with zero negotiation, and you skip the unpaid work of scoping and quoting from scratch every time. It turns buying into "yes/no to this menu item" instead of a drawn-out custom proposal — and you get faster and more profitable each time you run the same play.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q: Your SEO client's traffic is growing but they need leads this month for a new service launch. What's the natural upsell, and why?</summary>

Google Ads. SEO compounds slowly, but Ads buys instant visibility for that specific service right when they need it — it fills the gap SEO can't cover on a short timeline, while SEO keeps building underneath. And it's tied to a real, visible need, which is exactly how a legitimate upsell should work.
</details>

Growing the account is one lever. The other is charging more for what you already do — which means raising your rates and, sometimes, firing the clients dragging you down.
