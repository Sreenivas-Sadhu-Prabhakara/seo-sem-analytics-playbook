Every trade has a workshop. A tailor has a machine and a table; a cook has a stove and knives. Yours is a stack of free Google tools and a safe place to break things. And here's the part that surprises people: **the professional-grade kit for this entire job costs ₹0.** Google gives away the tools that run billion-rupee marketing operations, because they want more businesses advertising — and you get to borrow that same arsenal to learn on.

By the end of this lesson you'll have a clean setup to practise in for the rest of the course — without touching your personal Gmail and without spending anything.

## Why this pays

You cannot learn this craft by reading about it any more than you can learn to drive by reading the manual. You need a cockpit with real controls. Setting up your lab now means that when Module 4 says "create a GA4 property," you're not fumbling with account creation — you're already sitting in the seat, ready to work. A freelancer who can navigate these tools fluently looks ten times more credible to a client than one clicking around lost.

## Step zero: a dedicated Google account

Before anything else, **create a fresh, dedicated Google account** for your freelance work — something like `yourname.seo@gmail.com`. Do not run client marketing out of your personal Gmail full of family photos and food-delivery receipts.

Why this matters more than it sounds:

- **Separation.** Your work stays untangled from your personal life.
- **Access control.** You'll be sharing access to real client accounts. You want to grant and revoke that cleanly, not expose your personal inbox.
- **Professionalism.** "Please add my personal Gmail as admin" is not a sentence that inspires client confidence.

This one account becomes the hub that all your tools and your practice work hang off.

## The core free stack

Here's your workshop. Each of these is free, and each does one job. Don't try to master them today — just know what each one is *for*, so the name isn't scary when a later module hands you the keys.

| Tool | What it's for (one line) |
| --- | --- |
| **Google Business Profile** | The free local listing that puts a business on Search + Maps — the heart of local SEO. |
| **Google Analytics 4 (GA4)** | The scoreboard — measures who visits a site and what they do. |
| **Google Search Console** | Google's own report on how a site performs in search — what it ranks for, and what's broken. |
| **Google Ads (+ Keyword Planner)** | Runs paid search campaigns; Keyword Planner reveals what people search for and how often. |
| **Looker Studio** | Turns raw data into clean, client-friendly dashboards and reports (free). |
| **Google Trends** | Shows how search interest for a term rises, falls, and varies by region over time. |
| **PageSpeed Insights** | Grades how fast a page loads (critical on Indian mobile) and how to fix it. |
| **Google Tag Manager (GTM)** | A control panel for adding tracking (like GA4) to a site without editing code each time. |

That's the full professional kit. Ahrefs, Semrush, and the other paid heavyweights are lovely — and entirely optional, and for later. You can go a very long way, and get real clients real results, on this free stack alone. Don't let anyone tell you that you need a pricey monthly subscription to start. You need this list and the will to learn it.

:::caution[⚠️ Gotcha]
Keyword Planner technically lives inside a **Google Ads** account, and Google nudges you hard to enter billing details and launch a campaign during setup. You do **not** have to spend money to get an account and use Keyword Planner — look for the option to set up an account without a live campaign (sometimes called "expert mode" or skipping the campaign step), and don't enter card details unless you actually intend to run ads. Menus shift often; if you can't find the skip option, search Google's help for "create Google Ads account without campaign."
:::

## Your practice property: somewhere safe to break things

Tools are useless without something to point them at. You need a **practice property** — a real website you can attach GA4 and Search Console to and experiment on without fear.

You have a few honest options, roughly best to fallback:

1. **Your capstone client's site** (once you sign one in Module 1) — the ideal, because your practice *is* real work.
2. **A simple free site you spin up yourself** — a one-page site on a free host, or a free blog. It doesn't need to be pretty. It needs to be *yours*, so you can install tracking, watch the data flow, and make mistakes with zero stakes.
3. **A friend's or family member's small business site** — a low-pressure real-world sandbox.

The point is to have a place where, when a lesson says "now verify GA4 is receiving events," you have an actual site sending actual data, and you *see it work.* That "it's alive!" moment is when the abstract becomes real.

:::tip[🚀 Level up: free tiers of the "paid" tools]
When you're ready to go beyond Google's kit — still without paying — several premium tools offer genuinely useful free tiers:

- **Ahrefs Webmaster Tools (AWT)** — free backlink and site-audit data for a site you verify you own.
- **Bing Webmaster Tools** — free, and a second search engine's view of your site (with handy keyword and crawl data).
- **Microsoft Clarity** — free heatmaps and session recordings to *see* how visitors actually behave.

These are bonuses, not requirements. Nail the free Google stack first; add these when you're hungry for more.
:::

## Common mistakes

- **Using your personal Gmail** for client work. Untangle it now, thank yourself later.
- **Entering card details "just to finish setup"** on Google Ads when you only wanted Keyword Planner.
- **Collecting tools but never pointing them at anything.** Without a practice property, it stays theory.
- **Believing you need paid tools to start.** You don't. That myth mostly sells subscriptions.

## Do this now

Run the **Swipe this** checklist above, end to end. Create the dedicated account, reach all eight tools, and set up one practice property (even a bare one-page free site). When you're done, you have a working professional workshop — for ₹0 — that you'll use for the next eleven modules.
