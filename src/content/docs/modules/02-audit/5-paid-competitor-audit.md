The owner mentions, almost in passing, "oh, we boost on Google sometimes, my cousin runs it." You ask to see the account. Twenty minutes later you're staring at a campaign that's spent ₹40,000 over four months bidding on the single broad keyword "dental" — matching everyone from "dental college admission" to "dental assistant jobs" — with no negative keywords, no conversion tracking, and no idea whether a single rupee turned into a patient.

This is the most common paid-ads state you'll find at a small business: money on fire, no smoke alarm. This lesson teaches you to diagnose ad waste and read the competitive landscape — mostly for free, before you've spent a paisa of the client's money.

## Why this pays

Two reasons. First, wasted ad spend is the easiest ROI story in freelancing: "you're spending ₹40,000 a month and can't tell me if it works — I can fix that in week one" is nearly impossible to say no to. Second, the competitor read tells you and the client exactly what you're up against, which sizes the opportunity honestly. Module 9 goes deep on running and optimising ads; here you're the diagnostician sizing the problem and spotting the quick wins.

## Are they running Google Ads — and bleeding money?

If there's an existing account, get access (via your Ads manager account) and check the vitals. You don't need to be an Ads expert yet to spot a bleeding account — you need this short list of tell-tale wounds:

- **No conversion tracking.** The cardinal sin. If the account can't count calls, forms or WhatsApp leads, *nobody knows if any of the spend works.* Everything else is guessing.
- **No negative keywords.** Broad match with an empty negatives list means the client is paying for "jobs," "salary," "free," "course," "how to" and every other irrelevant search. Negatives are the cheapest fix in all of paid search.
- **Broad match with no guardrails.** One broad keyword and a big budget is a money bonfire.
- **Wrong location / radius targeting.** A Jayanagar clinic paying for clicks from Delhi.
- **Ads pointing at the homepage**, not a relevant landing page, so intent leaks away.
- **Running 24/7** when the business only answers the phone 10am–8pm — paying for midnight clicks nobody picks up.

:::note[💰 Rupee check]
How much do small accounts waste? There's no single honest statistic — anyone quoting "60% of ad spend is wasted" as gospel is guessing — but in the field it is *common* to find a meaningful chunk of a poorly-managed small account going to irrelevant searches, especially with no negatives and no conversion tracking. Illustratively, a business burning ₹30,000–₹60,000 a month on an unmanaged account can often get the same or more leads for less once negatives, tighter match types and conversion tracking are in. Frame it as "we can very likely do more with this budget," not a fabricated percentage. Underclaim, then over-deliver.
:::

If there's **no** account yet, your audit answers a different question: *is paid search even worth it here, and at what budget?* Some businesses (emergency plumber, dentist, lawyer) have high-intent, high-value searches where ads pay off fast. Others are better served by organic and GBP first. Say which, honestly.

:::caution[⚠️ Gotcha]
**Recommending ads to a client who can't convert them.** Before you cheer for paid search, remember the last two lessons: if the website is broken and nothing is tracked, ads pour hot traffic into a leaky, unmeasured bucket. Sequence matters. Sometimes the honest audit finding is "let's fix the landing page and turn on conversion tracking *before* we spend on ads." Recommending spend you know will leak is how you lose trust fast.
:::

## Read the competition — for free, from the search results

You don't need a paid tool to learn a lot about competitors. The search results page is a live intelligence feed if you know how to read it.

**1. Plain Google Search (incognito, on mobile, near the business).** Search the money keywords from your SEO audit. Note:

- **Who's buying ads** (the "Sponsored" results) vs who's ranking organically. A competitor who shows up in *both* is investing seriously.
- **What their ad copy promises** — price, offer, "open now," "20 years experience," free consultation. That copy is their tested pitch; you're reading their homework.
- **Who owns the Map Pack** — for local, the top-3 map results are often more valuable than the ads.

**2. The Ads Transparency Center.** Google's free **Ads Transparency Center** lets you look up an advertiser and see the ads they're currently running across Google. Search a competitor's business name and you can often see their live ad creatives and formats — real, current, and free. (Menus and coverage change, so if a competitor isn't listed, note it and move on rather than forcing it.)

**3. The competitor's own landing pages.** Click their ads. Where do they send traffic? A dedicated, fast, offer-led landing page vs their homepage tells you how sophisticated they are — and where you can beat them.

:::tip[🚀 Level up]
**Reading strategy from the SERP.** The search results page tells a story if you slow down. Lots of ads on a keyword = it's commercially valuable and competitive (someone's making money there). Ads plus a stuffed Map Pack plus review-heavy listings = a mature, expensive market — win it slowly with reviews and content, not a small ad budget. Thin results, weak competitor pages, no ads = an *underserved* keyword where you can win cheaply and fast. Teaching yourself to glance at a SERP and read "expensive/mature" vs "open/underserved" is a genuinely senior skill, and it directly shapes what you recommend: fight where you can win.
:::

## Estimating the opportunity — and sorting quick wins from long plays

You're not building a forecast model. You're making an honest, defensible call the owner can act on. For each opportunity, jot a rough **impact** (small/medium/large) and **effort** (quick/medium/long):

| Opportunity | Impact | Effort | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Add negative keywords + fix targeting on existing ads | Large | Quick | Quick win |
| Turn on conversion tracking | Large | Quick | Quick win |
| Point ads at a real landing page | Medium | Quick | Quick win |
| Build out reviews to match competitor | Large | Long | Long play |
| Rank organically for money keywords | Large | Long | Long play |

*(Illustrative example — your real table depends on the client.)*

**Quick wins** are the cheap, fast, visible fixes you do first to build trust and momentum (negatives, tracking, targeting). **Long plays** are the compounding investments — reviews, organic rankings, content — that take months but build a moat. A good audit gives the owner both: a fast "here's proof I'm worth it" win *and* a patient "here's how we win the market" plan. That combination is what turns an audit into a retainer.

:::tip[⚔️ War story]
A freelancer we'll call Priya (composite) inherited a Coimbatore gym's ad account spending roughly ₹1,000 a day on the broad keyword "fitness." Her first move wasn't a fancy strategy — it was adding a negatives list ("jobs," "certification," "trainer course," "free," "app") and tightening the location radius to the two neighbourhoods the gym actually served. Cost per lead dropped noticeably within two weeks, purely by *stopping waste*. She showed the owner the before/after in the audit report. That single quick win — subtraction, not addition — sold a six-month retainer. Sometimes the most impressive thing you can do is stop the bleeding.
:::

## Common mistakes

- **Judging ad performance with no conversion data** — if it isn't tracked, you literally cannot say if it "works."
- **Recommending ads before the site and tracking are fixed.** Sequence honestly.
- **Copying a competitor's strategy blindly** without knowing if *theirs* even works.
- **Inventing waste percentages.** Say "likely significant, here's why," not a made-up stat.

## Do this now

For your capstone client: if they run ads, list the top three wounds (missing negatives, no tracking, bad targeting, etc.). If they don't, decide and note whether paid search is worth it for them and at roughly what starting budget. Then run the money keywords through Google + the Ads Transparency Center and name the two competitors investing most heavily. Add one quick win and one long play to your opportunities list.

Offering both earns the immediate confidence *and* the multi-month retainer — the fast proof funds the patient strategy.
