Time to earn the module. You're going to produce the thing this whole stage was building toward: a **complete written Audit Report** for your capstone client — a document you could genuinely hand to a real business owner and use to sell the next phase of work.

This isn't a worksheet. It's the first real artefact in your freelance portfolio, and it defines everything you'll actually fix in Modules 4 through 9.

## The brief

Run a full diagnostic on your capstone client and package it into one structured, opportunity-framed report. The finished document must make an owner understand, in ten minutes, where they stand, what the biggest opportunities are, and what you recommend they do first — without a single line of unexplained jargon.

## Steps / checklist

Work through the module in order; each lesson fed one section of this report.

1. **Run the discovery interview.** Capture goals, best/most-profitable customers, seasonality, current lead sources, past marketing burns, and feared competitors. Write down their **ONE metric**.
2. **Complete each audit area using the checklists:**
   - **Technical quick-audit** — site exists, mobile, speed (PageSpeed Insights), indexed (`site:` + Search Console), HTTPS, broken things.
   - **SEO & local** — GBP state, money-keyword + "near me" rankings, on-page basics, service pages, content gaps, competitor comparison.
   - **Analytics & tracking** — GA4 present and *working*, Search Console, conversion tracking for calls/forms/WhatsApp, historical data, measurement gaps.
   - **Paid & competitor** — existing ad waste (or paid-search viability), competitor ads/rankings via Google + Ads Transparency Center, quick wins vs long plays.
3. **Prioritise findings** by impact and effort into quick wins, core projects, and long plays.
4. **Write the report** — executive summary (written last, placed first), findings by area with evidence and status colours, prioritised opportunities, a 30/60/90-day recommended plan, and illustrative costs.

## Deliverable format

A structured document (Google Doc, PDF, or slide deck) with these sections, in this order:

- **Executive summary** (half a page, plain language, top 3 opportunities + first step)
- **Findings by area** (four sections, each a short evidenced table with 🔴/⚠️/✅ status — and at least one asset you're marking ✅)
- **Prioritised opportunities** (quick wins / core / long plays)
- **Recommended plan** (phased, with what success looks like per phase)
- **Investment** (illustrative ranges, clearly labelled estimates)

Use screenshot **placeholders** where a visual belongs — don't fabricate images. Aim for something tight and skimmable, not a 40-page dump-truck.

:::note[📋 Swipe this]
**One-line self-test before you submit:** read only your executive summary aloud to someone who knows nothing about SEO. If they can repeat back where the business stands and what you'd do first, it's client-ready. If their eyes glaze, rewrite it.
:::

## Simulated Client fallback

No real capstone client yet? Use this **hypothetical composite** (clearly fictional — do not present it as a real business):

> **Glow & Go Unisex Salon — Kukatpally, Hyderabad.** Family-run for 9 years, two branches, ₹300–₹4,000 services (haircuts to bridal packages). Website: a slow, single-page `business.site` the owner's cousin made; not mobile-tested; no HTTPS on the custom domain they half-set-up. GBP: claimed but category set to "Beauty salon" only, 31 reviews averaging 4.3, last review 5 months ago, no owner replies, 4 photos from 2021. No GA4, no Search Console. They "boosted a post once" and ran a small Google Ads campaign on the keyword "salon" for a month with no tracking. Their ONE metric (from a mock discovery call): **new appointment bookings via phone and WhatsApp.** Feared competitor: "Blush Beauty Lounge," which owns the local Map Pack with 210 reviews and runs ads for "bridal makeup Hyderabad." Most-profitable customers: bridal and pre-wedding packages.

Produce the full Audit Report for Glow & Go exactly as you would for a paying client. Invent only realistic operational detail; keep all findings consistent with the facts above.

## Self-assessment rubric

Grade yourself honestly against each criterion.

| Criterion | Missing | Getting there | Client-ready |
|---|---|---|---|
| **All areas covered** | One or more audits skipped | All four present but shallow | Technical, SEO/local, analytics, paid all thoroughly covered |
| **Findings evidence-based** | Opinions, no proof | Some scores/screenshots | Every finding backed by a score, screenshot placeholder, or competitor number |
| **Prioritised** | Flat list of problems | Loosely grouped | Clear quick wins / core / long plays by impact & effort |
| **Opportunity-framed** | Doom-and-red throughout | Mixed tone | Every problem reframed as an opportunity; assets named; no naked jargon |
| **Client-ready presentation** | Rough notes | Structured but dense | Skimmable, exec summary sells it, costs shown as labelled ranges |

Anything below "Client-ready" in a row? Go back to that lesson's checklist and close the gap before you call it done.

## How this feeds your capstone

This report is the diagnosis that justifies the treatment. The prioritised opportunities become your work plan for the rest of the course — Module 4 wires up the measurement gaps you documented, Modules 5–8 close the SEO and local gaps, Module 9 fixes the ads. And because it's a real, professional deliverable, it's the **first portfolio piece** you can show the *next* prospective client to prove you don't guess — you diagnose. Keep it. You'll reuse this template for every client for the rest of your freelance career.
