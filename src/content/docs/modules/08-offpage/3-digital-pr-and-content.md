The prospecting list from the last lesson runs out eventually. You've claimed every supplier page and sponsored the run. Now what?

Now you flip the model. Instead of asking the internet to reflect relationships that already exist, you **give the internet a reason to point at your client that didn't exist before.** That's digital PR: create something genuinely worth referencing, then make sure the right people know it exists. Done right, one asset earns links for years.

## Why this pays

This is the highest-leverage, most defensible off-page work there is, which is exactly why you can charge properly for it. Anyone can email "please link to me." Very few freelancers can help a client *become linkable* — and that skill turns a link-building retainer from a grind into a compounding asset the client would be mad to cancel.

## Linkable assets: give people something to cite

A **linkable asset** is a page so genuinely useful, interesting or quotable that people link to it because it helps *their* audience — not as a favour to you. It's the "would this link exist if Google didn't?" test, passed on purpose.

For a local SMB, the winners are usually humble and hyper-specific:

- **The definitive local guide.** "The complete guide to running your first Bengaluru marathon (training, routes, injury prevention)" from a physio clinic. "Which school-uniform fabric survives a Chennai summer" from a boutique. Be *the* resource for one narrow local question and every blogger writing near that topic has a reason to cite you.
- **Original local data or a small survey.** Numbers are magnets — people cite data. A coaching centre could publish "We surveyed 200 local parents on board-exam stress." A restaurant could share "6 months of our most-ordered dishes by neighbourhood." Small, honest, *your own* data beats recycled statistics every time. (And because it's yours, you're never inventing figures — you're reporting them.)
- **A simple free tool or template.** A calculator, a checklist, a downloadable planner. A CA's "GST-deadline reminder calendar," a gym's "beginner 4-week home routine PDF." Utilities get bookmarked and linked.
- **A definitive resource / FAQ page** that genuinely out-helps everything else ranking for a local question.

:::tip[🚀 Level up]
**One linkable-asset idea per niche, to steal:**
- *Dentist:* "The honest cost guide to [treatment] in [city] — what actually drives the price."
- *Physio:* "Marathon-season injury guide for [city] runners."
- *Coaching centre:* an annual survey of local exam trends/stress, published as a mini-report.
- *Restaurant/café:* a genuinely good neighbourhood guide ("where to eat near [landmark]") that includes rivals — generosity gets linked.
- *Boutique:* a seasonal/festival styling guide grounded in local weather and occasions.
- *Real-estate/interiors:* "What ₹X actually buys you in [locality] right now," from real project data.

The pattern: pick the one question your client answers better than anyone in town, then answer it *definitively and locally*.
:::

## Newsjacking — the local angle

**Newsjacking** is attaching your client's expertise to something already in the news, fast. The national marathon season starts → your physio publishes an injury-prevention piece and pitches it to local sports writers *that week*. Board results are out → your coaching centre has a "what next" guide ready. Monsoon hits → the interiors client posts on damp-proofing.

The skill is timing and *localness*. National outlets have their own experts; the neighbourhood portal and the city blog are hungry for a credible local voice on a timely topic. Speed beats polish.

## Being a source: reporter-query services

Journalists and bloggers routinely put out calls for expert quotes. Answer one well and you get a quote plus (often) a link on a real publication.

:::caution[⚠️ Gotcha]
You may have heard of **HARO** (Help a Reporter Out) — the classic service for this. Be careful quoting it as live: HARO was wound down/rebranded and services in this space change often. The *tactic* is durable; the specific platform is not. Teach the goal ("get quoted as a local expert") and check what's currently active — options in this space have included Qwoted, Featured and SourceBottle, alongside simply building direct relationships with local journalists. Verify before you promise a client any named platform.
:::

For most Indian SMBs the most reliable version of this isn't a platform at all — it's **knowing three local journalists and community-page admins by name**, being genuinely helpful and fast when they need a quote, and never wasting their time. That relationship out-earns any tool.

## Outreach that isn't cringe

The reason most outreach fails: it's a mail-merge blast that screams "I want a link and I've sent this to 400 people." The fix is boring and effective — **personal, specific, value-first, and low-effort for them to say yes.**

:::note[📋 Swipe this]
**The non-cringe outreach email**

> **Subject:** A [city] injury guide for your marathon post
>
> Hi [First name],
>
> Real quick — I loved your piece on [specific thing they actually wrote]. The bit about [detail] was spot on.
>
> I help run Sunrise Physiotherapy here in Indiranagar, and we just put together a free guide on preventing common injuries for first-time [city] marathoners — routes, warm-ups, when to actually see a physio: [link].
>
> No ask attached — I just thought it might be useful for your readers around race season. If it's helpful and you ever want a local physio's quick quote for a piece, I'm one reply away.
>
> Cheers,
> [Name] · [phone/WhatsApp]

Why it works: you read their work (proof it's not a blast), you offer something genuinely useful, and you make the "no" easy — which paradoxically makes the "yes" more likely. Notice there's no "please add a dofollow link" line. You earn it or you don't.
:::

:::caution[⚠️ Gotcha]
Scaling the *exact same* templated email to hundreds of addresses is the fast lane to the spam folder and a burnt reputation. Ten researched, personalised emails beat two hundred merged ones — every single time. Outreach is a scalpel, not a fire hose.
:::

## Social proof feeds links and brand searches

Off-page isn't only links. A steady drip of social posts, happy-customer reposts, WhatsApp-status shout-outs and local Facebook-group mentions builds **brand searches** — people Googling the business by name. That's a strong signal of a real, prominent business, and it quietly primes bloggers and journalists to recognise the name when your pitch lands. Social rarely passes direct link credit, but it's the soil the links grow in.

:::tip[⚔️ War story]
A Pune café (composite) published a genuinely thorough "best filter coffee within 2 km" guide — and included two competitors, honestly rated. A city food blogger found it, called it the most useful local coffee post she'd seen, and linked it in her own roundup. Generosity got the link that self-promotion never would. The café didn't ask for anything; it just made something worth pointing at.
:::

## Common mistakes

- Building a "linkable asset" that's actually a thin sales page. If it doesn't help someone who'll never buy, it won't get linked.
- Inventing statistics to seem authoritative. Never. Use your own real data or cite the principle — a fake number is a career-ender the day someone checks.
- Outreach at volume with no personalisation. It doesn't scale trust; it scales annoyance.

## Do this now

Draft **one linkable-asset idea** for your capstone client — the one local question they can answer better than anyone in town — in a single sentence. Then write **one personalised outreach email** for a real recipient (a specific blogger, journalist or partner). Both go into your Assignment.

<details>
<summary>Q: What makes a page a "linkable asset" versus just another service page?</summary>

A linkable asset helps people who may never become customers — a guide, original data, a tool — so others cite it to serve *their own* audience. A service page mainly serves the sale, so no one else has a reason to link it. Usefulness to strangers is the test.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q: Why send ten researched emails instead of two hundred templated ones?</summary>

Because personalised, value-first outreach actually gets replies and links, while mass-templated blasts get ignored, marked as spam and can damage the sender's reputation. Off-page is about earning trust from real people; that doesn't scale by copy-paste.
</details>
