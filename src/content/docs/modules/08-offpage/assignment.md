Time to turn five lessons into a deliverable a client would happily pay a retainer against. You're going to produce a **Link & Reputation Plan** and actually *start* it — because a plan that never sends an email is just a nicely-formatted daydream.

## The brief

For your capstone client, deliver a short, practical document that:

1. **Assesses the current profile** — what already links to and mentions the business, and whether any of it is dodgy.
2. **Lists 10–15 realistic, relevant, mostly-local link and mention opportunities.**
3. **Includes one genuinely good outreach message** — personalised, value-first, not a mail-merge blast.
4. **Kicks off the first 3–5 outreaches** (sent, or queued and ready to send).
5. **Sets up reputation monitoring** so the client is never blindsided again.

Ethical only. If a tactic would fail the "would you show it to a Google engineer?" test, it doesn't go in the plan.

## Steps / checklist

1. **Assess current backlinks & mentions.**
   - Open **Google Search Console → Links** report; note the top linking sites and top linking text. Do they look relevant and real, or junky?
   - Check **Manual Actions** — confirm "No issues detected," or record what's there.
   - Optionally add a free backlink checker (Ahrefs Webmaster Tools free tier, Ahrefs free backlink checker, or Moz Link Explorer) for a second view. *(Free tiers are limited — verify current offerings.)*
   - Do one manual name search across Google, Maps, and the most relevant niche platform (Practo / Zomato / JustDial / IndiaMART etc.) to catch unlinked mentions and stray listings.
   - Write a 3–5 line honest summary: *"Current profile is mostly [X]; risks: [Y]; easiest wins: [Z]."*

2. **Build the opportunity list (10–15 rows).** Use the prospecting table from Lesson 2. Prioritise **relevance and locality**. Start with relationships that already exist (suppliers, referral partners, associations, one sponsorship), then add earned-media and linkable-asset ideas from Lesson 3. Include at least one **unlinked mention to convert** if your search found one.

3. **Craft one outreach message.** Personalised to a real, specific recipient. Value-first. Easy to say yes to. No "please add a dofollow link" line. Steal the templates from Lessons 3 and 4 and make them sound like a human.

4. **Send or queue the first 3–5.** Actually contact the easy wins — the supplier partner page, the association listing, the friendly unlinked-mention ask. Log the date and what you sent.

5. **Set up reputation monitoring.** Create **Google Alerts** for the business name (and owner's name if relevant), note the niche platforms to watch, and start the one-line monitoring log from Lesson 4.

## Deliverable format

A single document (Google Doc, Notion page or spreadsheet — whatever the client can open) containing:

- **The current-profile assessment** (short paragraph + Manual Actions status).
- **The opportunity list** — a 10–15 row table: opportunity, type, contact, the genuine reason they'd link/mention, priority, status.
- **The outreach message** — your one polished template, plus a log of the first 3–5 sent/queued.
- **Monitoring setup notes** — what alerts and searches are live, and the blank monitoring log ready to fill weekly.

## Simulated Client fallback (hypothetical — for readers with no real client yet)

**Composite client:** *Green Sprout Playschool & Daycare, Whitefield, Bengaluru.* A three-year-old preschool with a basic website, a decent Google Business Profile, and almost no other online presence. Parents choose it via word of mouth and local Facebook groups.

Build the full plan for Green Sprout. Ideas to spark you (find your own too): the local business chamber; the paediatric clinic and toy/book shop nearby (referral partners); sponsoring a colony kids' event or a nearby school fair; a genuinely useful **linkable asset** ("The Whitefield parents' guide to choosing a preschool — questions to ask, red flags, fees explained"); the Whitefield-parents Facebook group and city mommy-bloggers for mentions; and niche/local directories relevant to childcare. Monitor the name across Google, Maps and parenting community pages. Keep every tactic ethical and relevance-led.

## Self-assessment rubric

| Criterion | Missing | Getting there | Client-ready |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Current profile assessed** | Didn't check links/mentions | Glanced at GSC only | GSC Links + Manual Actions + name/niche search, honestly summarised |
| **Relevant opportunities** | Generic or irrelevant list | Some local ideas, thin on relevance | 10–15 relevant, mostly-local, prioritised opportunities |
| **Non-spammy outreach** | Templated blast / cringe | Semi-personalised, still salesy | Genuinely personal, value-first, easy to say yes to |
| **Outreach started** | Nothing sent | Drafted but not sent | First 3–5 sent or queued and logged |
| **Monitoring set up** | None | Alert created, no routine | Alerts + niche platforms + weekly log running |
| **Safety-aware** | Ignored risk | Noted risk vaguely | Checked Manual Actions, flagged any toxic links, no risky tactics in plan |

Aim for "Client-ready" in every row. If any row is still "Missing," that's your next hour of work — not a reason to ship.

## How this feeds your capstone

Authority compounds. On-page you fixed once; this plan you'll run for months, and even a handful of real local links plus a tidy, monitored reputation measurably moves a small business in a competitive local market. This document becomes a live workstream in your capstone engagement — and a portfolio centrepiece that proves you can build authority the durable, low-risk way. That's exactly the work clients keep paying for.
