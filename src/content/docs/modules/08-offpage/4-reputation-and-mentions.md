Someone in a Bengaluru runners' Facebook group posts: "Anyone know a good physio in Indiranagar? Knee's been killing me." Three people reply "Sunrise, near the metro — sorted my IT band."

Not one of those replies is a backlink. There's no `<a href>` anywhere. And yet that thread is worth more to the business than most links you'll ever build. Welcome to the reputation layer — the part of off-page that isn't about HTML at all, and the part clients feel most viscerally, because it's their *name* being talked about in public.

## Why this pays

Reputation work is the stickiest retainer in the book, because it never ends and it terrifies owners in the best way. Reviews keep coming, someone will eventually post something unfair, and the client wants a professional watching. Sell them peace of mind: you're the person who knows the moment their name comes up anywhere, good or bad, and knows what to do about it.

## Brand mentions: linked and unlinked

A **brand mention** is anyone naming the business online. Two flavours:

- **Linked mention** — the name is a clickable link. Great; that's a backlink too.
- **Unlinked mention** — the name appears as plain text: "we love Sunrise Physiotherapy" in a blog post, a forum, a news article, a WhatsApp-forwarded PDF.

Unlinked mentions matter more than beginners assume. They build name recognition across the web, they drive **branded searches** (people Googling the name — a strong signal of a real, prominent business), and — the practical bit — **every unlinked mention is a link waiting to be asked for.** Someone already said something nice with no prompting; converting that to a link is the easiest outreach you'll ever do.

:::note[🎓 Foundations]
Why does Google care about mentions and branded searches if they aren't links? Because they're hard to fake at scale and they correlate with a genuinely known, trusted business — exactly the "prominence" that helps local ranking. You can't easily counterfeit a town's word of mouth.
:::

## Monitoring the brand: your early-warning system

You can't manage what you can't see. Set up a simple monitoring routine so mentions come to you.

:::note[📋 Swipe this]
**The reputation-monitoring routine** (mostly free, ~20 minutes to set up, ~10 minutes a week after):

1. **Google Alerts** — create alerts for the exact business name (in quotes), common misspellings, and the owner's name if they're the face of the brand. Delivered to your inbox. Menus move; if a feature's missing, search Google's help for the current setup. *(Alerts catch newly-published web pages, not private social posts — cover those manually below.)*
2. **A weekly name search** — search the business name in Google, then in Google **Maps**, and skim what shows up. Repeat with a "near me"-style query for the service to see who's being recommended.
3. **Review platforms** — check the client's **Google Business Profile** reviews plus the niche platforms that matter for their category (more below). Set whatever native notifications each platform offers.
4. **Local social** — skim the relevant neighbourhood/city Facebook groups, and search the name on Instagram and X/Twitter. This is where the unlinked, un-alerted mentions live.
5. **A one-line log** — keep a simple sheet: date, where, what was said, link, action taken. That log *is* your monthly reputation report to the client.
:::

Paid tools (Semrush, Ahrefs, Brand24 and the like) automate mention-tracking if a client's scale justifies it, but for most SMBs the free routine above is enough. Don't upsell a tool the business doesn't need — that's how you lose trust.

## Consistency across profiles (NAP and beyond)

Your client is listed in more places than they realise — Google Business Profile, JustDial, Sulekha, Practo, Zomato, Facebook, industry directories. Every one is a mini-reputation surface. Two rules:

- **NAP consistency.** Name, Address, Phone must match *exactly* everywhere (this ties back to the local-SEO module). Mismatched listings confuse both customers and Google.
- **Same story everywhere.** Hours, services, photos and description shouldn't contradict across platforms. An abandoned JustDial listing with the old address and a dead number quietly leaks trust and customers.

## The niche platforms — the part everyone forgets

:::caution[⚠️ Gotcha]
Freelancers obsess over Google reviews and completely ignore the platform the client's customers *actually* use to decide. For a doctor or clinic, that's often **Practo**. For a restaurant, **Zomato** (and Google). For home services, **JustDial**, **Sulekha**, **UrbanCompany**. For a B2B supplier, **IndiaMART**. A five-star Google profile with a neglected, one-star-and-unanswered Practo page is a leak you didn't plug. Always ask: *"Where does someone in this category go to decide?"* — then go own that surface too.
:::

Claim the relevant niche profiles, keep them consistent, and — critically — **respond to reviews on them**, not just on Google. A calm, professional reply to a bad review on Practo or Zomato is read by dozens of future customers who never leave a review themselves. You're not arguing with the reviewer; you're performing professionalism for the silent audience that's deciding.

## Turning happy customers into advocates

The best reputation asset is a customer who'll vouch for the business unprompted. You engineer this gently:

- **Ask at the peak moment.** Right after a great result — the pain's gone, the biryani was perfect — is when a review request lands. A short, friendly WhatsApp with a direct link to leave a Google review removes all friction. Never buy or fake reviews; incentivising or faking them violates platform policies and Google's guidelines, and gets profiles suspended.
- **Make sharing easy.** A tasteful "tag us / share your progress" nudge turns a happy customer into a social mention.
- **Feature them (with consent).** A reposted testimonial or before/after — permission first, always — thanks the customer and seeds more social proof.

:::tip[⚔️ War story]
A Hyderabad diagnostics lab (composite) had glowing Google reviews and had never once looked at Practo, where an unanswered two-star complaint sat at the top of their profile for months. Patients were reading it and quietly booking elsewhere. The freelancer claimed the page, replied to the complaint calmly, resolved the person's issue offline, and started a gentle review-ask routine. Within weeks the Practo rating recovered — because the leak was never the lab's service, only the platform nobody was watching.
:::

## Converting unlinked mentions into links

This is the highest-ROI outreach in the whole module, because the hard part — earning the goodwill — is already done. When your monitoring turns up a plain-text mention on a real site:

:::note[📋 Swipe this]
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thank you so much for the kind mention of [Business] in your post on [topic] — genuinely made our week.
>
> Tiny request if it's easy: would you mind linking our name to our site ([URL]) so your readers can find us directly? No worries at all if not — either way, thank you for the shout-out.
>
> Warmly, [Name]
:::

They already like the business. Half the time they simply forgot to link, and are happy to fix it. This is the friendliest email you'll send all month.

## Common mistakes

- Watching only Google and ignoring the niche platform where the category actually gets chosen.
- Replying to good reviews but ghosting the bad ones — the bad ones are the ones future customers read most closely.
- Letting stale, inconsistent listings rot across platforms.
- Chasing shortcuts on reviews. Fake or incentivised reviews are a policy violation and a suspension risk. Don't.

## Do this now

For your capstone client, set up **Google Alerts** for the business name and run one **manual name search** across Google, Maps and the single most relevant niche platform for their category. Log what you find in one row of your monitoring sheet. That sheet is part of your Assignment.

<details>
<summary>Q: Why chase an unlinked mention instead of building a brand-new link from scratch?</summary>

Because the goodwill already exists — someone chose to mention the business unprompted, so a friendly "would you mind linking it?" has a high hit rate. It's far less effort than earning a cold link, and even if they don't link, the unlinked mention already builds brand awareness and branded searches.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q: A clinic has 4.8 stars on Google but ignores Practo. What's the risk?</summary>

Patients in that category often decide on Practo, not Google — so an unmanaged, poorly-reviewed or unanswered Practo profile can quietly cost bookings even while Google looks great. Reputation lives on whichever platform your client's customers actually use to choose; you have to monitor and manage all of them.
</details>
