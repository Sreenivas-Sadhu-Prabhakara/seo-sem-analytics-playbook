Two people type "braces" into Google. One is a 22-year-old comparing clinics and prices for their overbite tonight. The other is a Class 9 student writing a science project on orthodontic wire. Same word. Wildly different wants. If Meena's clinic ranks a chirpy blog post titled "The History of Braces" and beats the service page, congratulations — you're educating schoolkids for free and losing the patient who was ready to book.

That's **search intent**: the *why* behind the *what*. Master it and you stop optimising for words and start optimising for humans who are about to spend money.

## Why this pays

Ranking is worthless if you rank the wrong *kind* of page for a keyword. Intent is the bridge between the keyword map you built last lesson and the pages you'll build later. It decides whether a term deserves a blog post, a service page, a Google Business Profile action, or an ad — and getting that call right is the difference between traffic and leads.

## The four intents (plus one that rules India)

Every query leans toward one of four intents:

| Intent | What they want | Example (dental) | Right response |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Informational** | An answer, to learn | "why does a root canal hurt" | Blog / FAQ |
| **Navigational** | A specific site/brand | "meena dental clinic jayanagar" | Your homepage / GBP |
| **Commercial** (investigation) | To compare before buying | "best dentist in jayanagar," "root canal cost" | Service page, reviews, comparison |
| **Transactional** | To act *now* | "book dentist appointment near me," "emergency dentist open now" | Service page + booking / call, ads |

And the one that reshapes everything for local SMBs:

- **Local intent** — the searcher wants something *near them, now*. "Dentist near me," "gym close by," "biryani home delivery." Google often infers this even without the words "near me," using the user's location. Local intent usually rides on top of commercial or transactional intent and points hard at your **Google Business Profile** and Maps, not just the website.

:::note[🎓 Foundations]
**Intent > keyword.** A keyword is text; intent is the human need behind it. "Cheap dentist" and "affordable dental clinic" are near-identical intent (price-conscious, ready-ish to buy) despite different words. Group by intent, not by spelling — that's how you decide what to build.
:::

## Intent dictates the page type

This is the practical payoff. Once you know a term's intent, the page almost writes itself:

- **Informational** → a blog post or FAQ. Great for building topical authority and trust, weak for immediate leads. Don't force a "Book Now" — answer the question, then offer a soft next step.
- **Commercial** → a strong **service page** with proof: prices or price ranges, before/afters, reviews, credentials. This is where comparison shoppers get convinced.
- **Transactional / local** → the page that makes acting trivial: click-to-call, WhatsApp button, booking, directions, hours. For "near me" and "open now," your **GBP** often does more heavy lifting than the website.
- **Navigational** → make sure your homepage and GBP own your own brand name (you'd be amazed how many businesses lose their own name to JustDial listings).

:::tip[⚔️ War story]
A freelancer (composite, but you'll recognise the type) landed a coaching centre and went all-in on content. He wrote a beautiful 2,000-word guide, "How to Prepare for NEET," and ranked it on page one for "how to prepare for NEET." Traffic soared. The owner was thrilled — for a month. Then he asked the obvious question: *how many admissions?* Zero. Every visitor was a student wanting free study tips, not a parent ready to pay ₹80,000 for coaching. He'd ranked #1 for pure informational intent and starved the bottom-funnel pages that actually convert. He'd optimised the *word* and ignored the *want*. The centre didn't renew.
:::

## Read the SERP to infer intent (the free cheat code)

You don't have to *guess* intent. Google already did the research — it shows the page types that win. **Type the keyword and read the results:**

- Mostly **blog posts, "how-to," Wikipedia**? → informational. A service page won't rank; don't fight it.
- **Service pages, product listings, "best of" comparisons, ads on top**? → commercial/transactional. Money is moving here.
- A **Map pack** (the local 3-pack with pins)? → strong local intent. You compete via GBP, reviews, and proximity, not just blue links.
- **Shopping ads, prices**? → transactional.

If the SERP shows blog posts and you built a service page, you'll lose — and vice versa. Match the format Google is already rewarding. This one habit saves months.

## Map keywords to the buyer journey

Every searcher is somewhere on a path: **aware → considering → ready-to-buy.**

- **Aware (top-funnel):** "do I need a root canal or extraction" — learning, not buying.
- **Considering (mid-funnel):** "root canal vs extraction cost," "best dental clinic jayanagar" — comparing.
- **Ready-to-buy (bottom-funnel):** "root canal jayanagar," "emergency dentist near me open now" — pull out the wallet.

For a small business with limited time and budget, the sequencing is almost always the same: **win the bottom of the funnel first.** Those searchers are the closest to revenue, the terms are usually more specific (and cheaper to compete for), and early wins buy you the client's trust — and their patience for the slower top-funnel content later.

:::caution[⚠️ Gotcha]
Don't let a client (or your own ego) drag you into top-funnel content before the money terms are handled. "Let's start a blog!" is a lovely instinct that produces traffic-shaped vanity and no patients for six months. Cover the bottom-funnel, lead-generating terms first; earn the right to publish thought-leadership later.
:::

:::tip[🚀 Level up]
**SERP features are intent signals — read them like a menu.** The extras Google bolts onto a results page tell you what it thinks searchers want, and how you can win the space:
- **Map pack** → local intent; GBP + reviews + proximity are your levers.
- **People Also Ask** → question intent; structure content as clear Q&A to earn a slot.
- **Featured snippet / "answer box"** → informational; a concise, well-structured answer paragraph can capture it.
- **Shopping / price boxes** → transactional; often a job for ads.
- **Lots of ads crammed up top** → commercially valuable term; organic will be a grind, so consider running ads *while* SEO matures.
Reading features is free competitive intelligence — do it before you promise a client anything.
:::

## Common mistakes

- Building one page and hoping it satisfies informational *and* transactional searchers — it satisfies neither.
- Ignoring the Map pack and optimising only for blue-link rankings on local terms.
- Guessing intent from the words instead of *reading the live SERP*.
- Front-loading blog content when the client needs leads this quarter.

## Do this now

Take 10 keywords from your capstone client's sheet. Add two columns: **Intent** (informational / commercial / transactional / navigational, and flag local) and **Journey stage** (aware / considering / ready). For any you're unsure about, actually search the term and note what page types rank. You now know which keywords to prioritise — and which page each one deserves.

<details>
<summary>Q: A keyword has great volume, but every result on page one is a blog post. Should you build a service page for it?</summary>

No. The SERP is telling you Google reads that term as informational — a service page won't rank, and even if it did, the searchers aren't ready to buy. Either satisfy the intent with content, or skip the term and spend your effort on a commercial/transactional keyword instead.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q: Why do most small businesses prioritise bottom-funnel keywords first?</summary>

Because ready-to-buy searchers are closest to revenue, bottom-funnel terms are usually more specific and less competitive, and early lead-generating wins build the client's trust and buy you patience for slower top-funnel content later.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Q: The word "near me" isn't in the query — can there still be local intent?</summary>

Yes. Google infers local intent from the user's location and the type of query (a dentist, a gym, a restaurant), often showing a Map pack even without "near me" in the words. That's why you optimise the Google Business Profile for a whole category of searches, not just literal "near me" phrases.
</details>
