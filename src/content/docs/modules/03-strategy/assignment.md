This is the module's real deliverable — and it's not a classroom exercise. It's the exact artefact you'd put in front of a paying owner to get a signature before any execution begins. Everything you learned in this module converges here into two things a client can hold: a **keyword map** and a **strategy document**. This roadmap becomes the marching orders for Modules 4 through 9.

## The brief

For your **capstone client** (real, if you have one; the simulated client below if you don't), produce:

1. A **keyword map** — a structured table of the keywords worth targeting, each tagged with intent, target page, and priority.
2. A **strategy document** — a skimmable, few-page plan with goals, target themes, a channel plan, a 90-day roadmap, a measurement plan, and the investment — ending in an explicit approval close.

Together these are what you'd send to win sign-off. Treat it that way.

## Steps / checklist

**1. Build the keyword list.**
- Harvest seeds from the business + your audit + how customers actually talk.
- Expand with Google Keyword Planner (India location), autocomplete, "People also ask," related searches, and Google Trends for seasonality.
- Run the local-expansion trick: service × locality × landmark. Add Hinglish/transliterated variants, validated in autocomplete.
- Cluster everything into 4–8 **themes**.

**2. Map intent and target.**
- Tag each keyword's **intent** (informational / navigational / commercial / transactional; flag local).
- Assign each a **target page** — a specific service page, blog/FAQ, the homepage, or the **Google Business Profile**.
- Where unsure, read the live SERP to confirm.

**3. Prioritise with the matrix.**
- Score each opportunity (and each audit fix) on impact vs effort.
- Assign a **priority** and rough sequence — quick wins first.

**4. Set honest goals + baseline plan.**
- Write one lead/revenue-tied goal with a **range** and **timeframe** (never a guaranteed ranking).
- Name the baseline you'll capture in Module 4; if you don't have numbers yet, mark them "baseline TBD."

**5. Write the strategy doc.**
- Follow the lesson-6 outline: goals → baseline → target keywords/themes → channel plan (Local SEO / on-page / ads) → 90-day roadmap → measurement plan → investment → approval close.
- Rewrite every tactic as a **business outcome**. Keep it skimmable.

## Deliverable format

**Keyword map** — a spreadsheet or table with (at minimum) these columns:

| Keyword | Theme | Intent | Local? | Target page | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| root canal jayanagar | Root canal | Transactional | Yes | /root-canal service page | High (Week 1–2) |
| teeth whitening cost bangalore | Whitening | Commercial | No | /teeth-whitening page | Medium |
| dentist near forum mall | Emergency/local | Transactional | Yes | Google Business Profile | High |
| why does a root canal hurt | Root canal | Informational | No | Blog / FAQ | Low |

*(Rows above are illustrative composites — replace with your client's real terms.)*

**Strategy document** — a tight, few-page doc (a shared doc or PDF), leading with outcomes, tables over prose, the goal/timeline/investment bolded, and an explicit "approve to begin" line at the end.

## Simulated Client fallback

*(Hypothetical — use this only if you have no real client. It's a composite, not a real business.)*

**Fit Forge Gym, JP Nagar, Bengaluru.** A single-branch gym, three years old, ₹1,800/month memberships. Owner Rajesh runs it hands-on. The website is a one-page brochure; the Google Business Profile exists but is half-empty with last year's hours and no photos. Two national chain gyms and a boutique CrossFit studio are within 3 km. New-member enquiries come mostly by word of mouth and the odd JustDial call — Rajesh estimates ~15 enquiries a month and wants more, especially "people nearby who are actually ready to join." He has a modest monthly marketing budget and zero patience for jargon. Build the full keyword map and strategy doc for Fit Forge as if pitching Rajesh — remember January is *not* a reliable membership-spike month in India the way it is in the West, so don't build your seasonality plan on that assumption.

## Self-assessment rubric

| Criterion | Missing | Getting there | Client-ready |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Keywords researched** | A short guessed list, one source | Used tools, some breadth | Seeds + Planner + autocomplete + Trends, clustered into clear themes |
| **Intent mapped** | No intent tagging | Intent guessed from words | Intent tagged, local flagged, SERP-checked where unsure |
| **Local variants included** | English-only, generic | Some locality terms | Landmark + locality + Hinglish/transliterated variants, autocomplete-validated |
| **Prioritised** | Flat list, no order | Rough sense of priority | Scored on effort/impact, quick wins first, sequenced across 90 days |
| **Honest goals** | Ranking guarantee or none | A goal, but vague/no baseline | Lead/revenue goal, range + timeframe, no guarantees, baseline named |
| **Clear roadmap** | No plan/tactics only | A list of tasks | Skimmable doc, outcome-framed, 90-day roadmap, measurement, investment, approval close |

Grade yourself honestly. Anything short of "Client-ready" in a row is a to-do, not a verdict — fix it before you'd send this to a real owner.

## How this feeds your capstone

This is the hinge of the whole course. The keyword map tells every later module *what to target*; the roadmap tells them *in what order*. Module 4 captures the baselines your goals reference; Modules 5–9 execute the channel plan line by line — local SEO, on-page, technical, off-page, and ads. When you finish the course, this document is the plan you actually delivered on. Build it like the client already paid for it. Because in the real version, they did.
